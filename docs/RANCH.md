# Thistledown: design

A first-person creature ranch, in the genre of games like Slime Rancher. Everything here is original: the creatures, the tool, the world, the names and the art. No slimes, no plorts, no vacuum gun, nothing copied.

**The fantasy:** you run a ranch for downies, round fluffy creatures like living dandelion clocks. You roam a soft, windy valley with your bellows, a hand-cranked wind tool that draws things in and puffs them out. You catch downies, carry them home, pen them and feed them. Fed downies shed down, which you sell at the Exchange. Coins buy pens, gardens, upgrades, and gates to the far meadows where rarer downies live.

The core feel is hands-on, tactile and a little chaotic. Drawing a cloud of fluff, berries and a bouncing downy into the canister, then puffing them across a field, should be a joy in itself.

## World

One continuous outdoor map, about 160 by 120 m, with gentle heightfield hills. Gates between zones open with coins.

- **The Ranch** (home, start): a plateau with the ranch house, the Exchange crate with its price board, six build pads in two rows, and one starter garden already built with clover planted. The workbench for bellows upgrades is at the house.
- **Clover Meadow** (open from the start): rolling grass, a stream, wild clover patches that regrow clover heads, and dewberry bushes. Clover downies live here.
- **Ember Ridge** (gate, 500 coins): warm rocky hills, steam vents and wild sweetroot. Ember downies live here, and thistle downies on the ridge top at dusk.
- **Brook Hollow** (gate, 1,200 coins): a marshy pond basin with reeds, lily pads and dewberry thickets. Brook downies live here.

**Days:** a day lasts 12 real minutes, with dawn, day, dusk and night. Night is darker and bluer, the downies glow faintly, and nettlebacks roam. Sleeping in the house skips to morning and saves.

## The bellows

- **Draw** (hold): pulls food and down tufts in a cone, 7 m long with a 25-degree half angle; they accelerate toward the muzzle and go into the canister when within about 1 m, quickly (a garden's worth in a second or two). **Downies come only when the crosshair is on them** (within reach, the aim inside their body with a little give for thumbs: `aimedDowny` in `sim.ts`), never because they stood inside the cone beside the fluff you were pulling; food or fluff nearer the crosshair wins, **except over a wild downy already tugging against you**, and while a wild one fights the pull (or comes in) the cone takes no food or fluff at all: anything on its way drops. (The fourth playtest's careful pulls at a brook drew dewberries in 3 tries of 4 and the jar filled to 20.) Your loose downies come at once; **a penned one comes only when aimed at directly** (no thumb give, fluff near the crosshair wins by a wider margin), **never while food or fluff is in the cone, nor for 0.6 s after** (`SWEPT`), and only to an aim held still on it (turning under 0.9 rad/s, `STEADY`) for 0.45 s (`PEN_TUG`). So pulling up a pen's fluff never takes a downy, even one standing in front of the fluff: the third playtest's sweeps took one in 3 of about 9 (a test aiming through a downy at the fluff behind it took 4 of 9 on that code, none now). **A press of Pull that started aimed at food or fluff (within reach or up to 4 m past it, `PRESS_LOOK`), or that has drawn any in, never takes a penned downy** until it is let go, unless the press started squarely on that downy in front of the food (`aimedAtThing`). The fifth playtest's pulls at a pen's far-side fluff, out of reach behind a downy, took the downy in 2 of 4 (its repro: 2 of 6 with the fluff out of reach, 0 of 6 within it): Pull reaches 7 m and a pen is 8.8 m across, so the crosshair held still past a hopping downy. **A pen's fluff now gathers by the fence on the lane side, beside the post** (`GATHER`, `gatherSpot`; a pen with a chute still sends it to the box): lying 0.6 s, it drifts there at 0.9 m/s, so the far side's fluff comes within reach from the lane (a tuft on the far side, 9.6 m from the post, lies 5 m from it 15 s later). The porch downy digs in for 0.35 s and only dodges a little. Nettlebacks are too prickly and can't be drawn.
- **Each kind fights in its own way** (`CATCH` in `sim.ts`), so going out is a skill (the third playtest's thumb with 180 ms of lag caught 8 of 8; the fourth's careless thumb still caught 4 embers of 4, a second pull straight after the first). A wild one digs in while it creeps your way and a ring round the crosshair fills; how far off its body the crosshair may be and still hold it is its kind's give (a downy already tugging has 30% more). **Its jinks never head at you**: a glide goes sideways or away, never under the view (the fourth playtest's brook glided under it at 0.8 m, then dove), and a brook diving for the pond goes round you, not past your face.
  - **Clover**: 0.9 s of pull, quick sideways hops (2.6 to 3.6 m/s every 0.3 to 0.55 s), 0.2 m of give, the most of any kind but the thistle: the gentle first catch (it was 0.1, the tightest: the fifth playtest's careless real-touch thumb caught 0 of 6).
  - **Ember**: 1.0 s, it darts: a fast low leap (4.4 to 5.8 m/s; it was 5 to 6.4) every 0.4 to 0.65 s, **0.13 m of give** (it was 0.17: the sixth playtest's careless thumb caught 10 embers of 10, "catching is rote"); lose it and it is off, but a slipped pull fades slower than the others' (`fade` 1.05 a second against 1.5), so a dart gives you a moment to find it again. It wants a steady thumb now: see the numbers below.
  - **The board's rare one** fights as its kind does, harder (`RARE_FIGHT`): 1.2 times as long to bring in, with 0.9 of its kind's give, and a rare brook never dives. **Pulled at unlured, it slips the pull in 1.2 s** (`fightLimit`, `RARE_WARY.slip`) and bolts: it is to be lured, not chased (see the Exchange).
  - **Brook**: 1.0 s, slippery (0.16 m of give; it was 0.14), it slips off towards the pond in quick glides, and if the pull is not done within 3.2 s (it was 2.5) it slips into the water and is gone. **The tug ring shows its time** (`tugLeft`): an outer ring round the crosshair, pale blue on a dark ink track the whole way round, shrinking as the time runs out and warm for its last third (the fifth playtest's brooks dove after about 3 s with nothing to say so). It is 45 px across, its arc 7.8 to 8.9 times the contrast of its track (the sixth playtest's was 37 px, pale blue on green at 2.6 to 1), and the count badge steps out 5 px clear of it while it shows (it touched).
  - **Thistle**: it hears you from 12 m, walking or running (and from above or below), and **bolts**: long flat leaps faster than you walk, choosing the way that takes it furthest from you without climbing, then settling where it fled to. **After 3 s of bolting it is winded** for 4 s (`ESCAPE.boltFor`, `ESCAPE.winded`; they were 4 and 3): ordinary hops, so a walker who keeps after it closes in, a runner sooner. Up close it needs a quick tug: 0.7 s of pull within 2.1 s (it was 1.7; 0.35 m of give), its time on the tug ring like a brook's.
  - **How it gets away** depends on the kind. **One with a time** (a brook, a thistle) breaks free only when its time runs out: a pull that lapses starts the fight over (the fifth playtest's careless thumb lost all ten brooks to a lapse, each into the pond). **One without** (a clover, an ember) gets away if the pull lapses once it was a third of the way in (`SHAKE_LOOSE`). Either way: a long leap away (a brook into the pond), the bellows refused for 1.6 s (`GOT_AWAY`), and a note under the crosshair, "It got away." or "It slipped into the water.". **A getaway costs a chase** (`ESCAPE`): it runs flat out for 3.5 s, and for 10 s it is wary, hearing you from 12 m and digging in half as long again.
  - **Food never beats a wild one under the crosshair**: one already tugging keeps the pull, and so does one with the crosshair on its very body; food nearer the crosshair wins only off its body (the fifth playtest's careful pulls at embers drew sweetroot in 3 of 8).
  - **Running is taught** (the `run` lesson, below): the fifth playtest never learned to sprint, and its walking thumb stayed 8 m off a fleeing clover. In the sim a walker from 10 m is within Pull's reach of a clover that has seen it in 12 tries of 12 (middle 1.1 s; running, 0.6 s), so walking was left as it is.
  - Measured by `npx tsx scripts/ranch-catch.ts`, which plays the way the fourth playtest's real-touch chase did (80 tries per kind and thumb): from 12 m on any dry side (16 m from a thistle, at dusk), the valley's wild food lying about, walking in to 5.5 m and holding Pull within 7.6 m, the view moved once every 40 ms (lag, gain up to 60 px a move, wobble), the sim at 60 frames a second, and a downy that got away chased again within 14 s. **Its thumbs now carry the real loop's lag** (`LOOP_LAG`, 100 ms: the touch event, a frame, the page), without which it ran far kinder than real touch (careless clover 100% here against 50%). Round 5, careless (280 ms of lag, 55% a move, 6 px of wobble, walking): clover 53%, ember 55%, brook 53%, thistle 16%; careful (170 ms, 80%, 1.5 px, running at a thistle): 100%, 86%, 95%, 90%. **`node scripts/ranch-catch-real.mjs` is the measure that counts** (real CDP touch on the Pixel's viewport, 20 tries per kind and thumb, 60 fps): careless clover 13 of 20, ember 11, brook 10, thistle 6 (the thistle swings from run to run: 8, 8, 3 and 6 of 20 on near builds); careful 20, 19, 18 and 18 of 20. The playtest's build, the same harness: careless 0, 3, 1 and 1 of 6, careful 6, 4, 6, 6. The thistle's careless walker stays under the target band (about 30% pooled): it never runs, and running is now taught; a thistle is the dusk catch, in no order. **Round 6** (the sixth playtest's careless thumb caught 18 clovers of 19 and 10 embers of 10: "catching is rote"), with the ember's give at 0.13 and the rare one's harder fight: the sim harness, 120 tries, careless clover 45%, ember 43% (55% before), brook 57%, thistle 21%; careful 100%, 83%, 97%, 90%. The rare one lured (from 8 m, walking in to 4 m): careless 29%, 33%, 34% for a gold clover, rose ember and silver brook, careful 100%, 60%, 89%; not lured, a careful thumb walking in from 12 m catches none. Real touch (`ranch-catch-real.mjs`, 16 tries, 60 fps): careless clover 8 of 16, ember 7 (11 of 20 before), brook 10; careful 16, 15 and 14; the lured rare clover careless 7, careful 16; the lured rare ember careless 5, careful 14. Clover stays the gentle catch; the ember and the rare one want a steady thumb.
- **The crosshair marks only what is under it and within Pull's reach** (7.4 m): a downy you are aimed at, or food or fluff within about 0.45 m of the crosshair's line. It no longer shows the mark for something at the cone's edge while you aim at a downy 8 m off.
- **A downy keeps its hunger through the jar** (`DownRec` in state.ts: its hunger, the day it was caught and whether it is the rare one, kept per downy and saved): the fifth playtest's 0.06 came out 0.37 and its 0.80 came out 0.35.
- **Canister:** 4 slots of 12 each. Each slot holds one kind of thing, except that **one window takes every kind of fluff and holds twice as much** (`FLUFF_ROOM`; 24, 40, 60 with the bigger jars): fluff is light. The fourth playtest's four windows held three foods and a fluff, and 11 ember and 8 thistle fluff lay unpulled while an ember order missed at 10 of 14. The fluff window's kinds come out oldest first (the jar shows each wisp in its kind's colour, the count says "fluff" once it holds more than one kind), so a held Blow sends every kind into the crate on one press, and tipping it out drops every kind in it. A full canister refuses new kinds, with a clear "full" cue and a note saying how to make room. **A whole window tips out**, tossed 2 to 5 m ahead (it used to land within 1.7 m and the next pull swept it straight back in), and the bellows leaves what tipped out alone for 1.6 s (`TIP_IGNORE`): hold the window (touch), hold its number key, or hold Q (keys) or LB or RB (pad) for about half a second to empty the chosen one. A quick Q, R, LB or RB press (or the wheel, or E with nothing in reach) steps to the next window **that holds something, skipping empty ones** (`Sim.stepWindow`; the third playtest's Q stepped through empty windows and a Blow mid-step sent clover heads at the crate). Things tipped out are still yours: food to feed with, and fluff the sell crate takes counts as your sale.
- **Puff** (tap): launches one of the selected slot's item at about 14 m/s on an arc. Holding puff repeats about 4 times a second. **When the chosen window runs out, the next window that holds something is chosen** (and a held Blow waits for a fresh press before sending the next kind). **With nothing aboard, Blow still pushes a puff of air**: a pest within 5 m in front of you goes rolling off (`AIR_REACH`; the sixth playtest's empty Blow at a pest 3.5 m off did nothing), and light things are shoved about. Only if it moved no pest does it say "Nothing in the jar." (the second playtest's Blow did nothing after a sale emptied the chosen window, and nothing said why), and **the note goes as soon as something comes aboard** (the sixth playtest's stayed up over 19 fluff).
- **Slots:** select with 1 to 4, the wheel, or Q and E.
- **Upgrades at the workbench:**

  | Slots | 4 to 5 | 800 |
  | Capacity | 12 to 20 to 30 | 400, 1,200 |
  | Range | 7 to 10 m | 600 |

## Downies

Downies are round puff bodies about 0.5 m across, with a seed-head fluff shell, tiny feet and big friendly eyes. They hop, bump, squash on landing, and drift slowly when airborne (low gravity, high drag).

| Kind | Look | Home | Favourite | Also eats | Down value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clover | green and cream | Meadow | clover heads | any greens | 12 |
| Ember | amber, soft inner glow | Ridge | sweetroot | any roots | 20 |
| Brook | blue, glossy | Hollow | dewberries | any fruit | 26 |
| Thistle | violet, prickly tufts, rare | Ridge top at dusk | anything | anything | 60 |

**Food** comes in three types: greens (clover heads), roots (sweetroot) and fruit (dewberries).

**Behaviour:**
- **Hunger:** rises from 0 to 1 over about 3 game hours in the open, and a third as fast in a pen (`PEN_HUNGER`): a penned downy is hungry about 4.5 game hours (2.25 real minutes) after a meal. **A penned downy already hungry waits patiently**: past half hungry its hunger runs at half that rate (`PEN_PATIENT`), and **at night (9 pm to 5 am) it dozes**, half again (`PEN_DOZE`). So a pen goes hungry as often as before by day (the same meals and fluff), but is starving 13.5 game hours after a meal rather than 9, and a night with no feeder no longer starves it (the sixth playtest's three clovers woke at hunger 1.0 after one night, one over the fence). Above 0.5 a downy looks for food it can see within 10 m (a penned one, inside its pen) and heads straight for it, cutting its slow hungry bounce short; a starving one (hunger 1) hurries, with quicker, longer hops. Neither ever hops away from food it has seen. Food still flying from a blow is not seen until it lands; food dropping from a feeder or a crop is.
- **Hungry downies show it:** a hungry downy of yours has its favourite food bobbing just over its head (clover for a thistle, which eats anything), so hunger and the right food are shown in the world. A lesson's marker over the same downy stands in for its sign.
- **Shedding:** eating its favourite sheds 2 down tufts; other food it eats sheds 1, however hungry it was. It won't eat a food type it doesn't eat. Only fright and a crowded pen stop it shedding. **Only downies you keep shed**: a wild downy's meal makes no fluff, so the meadow is no field of free fluff (the second playtest found 70 to 84 lying there by mid-afternoon and swept up about eight times what ranching paid).
- **Moods:**
  - **Content:** recently fed.
  - **Hungry:** big eyes, a slower bounce (starving looks the same: hunger alone never frazzles).
  - **Frazzled:** crowded (more than 10 in one pen, `PEN_MAX`; taught before it bites, see the lessons), a nettleback nearby, or long neglect (starving for 4 game hours, `NEGLECT_HOURS`). **A fright in a pen sends a downy running round inside the fence** in quick, ordinary hops; only a crowded pen or long neglect makes one hop twice as high and clear a low fence (never on its way to food). A frightened or crowded one doesn't shed. A fed, calm downy never leaves a low fence: in the tests a penned downy left with no food at all goes over after 6 to 15 minutes, not 2.5, and a fed one frightened for two minutes beside a stocked feeder stays in (the second playtest's went over with 10 food in the feeder).
- **Wild downies notice you:** within 9 m (12 m when you run, and always for a thistle; `NOTICE`, `NOTICE_RUN`), further than Pull reaches, a wild one squeaks and hops away from you (a thistle bolts), so catching is a skill: close in on one that has seen you, then hold the crosshair on it through its kind's fight (see the bellows). The board's rare one is warier still: 13 m walking, 20 m running (see the Exchange). Yours and the porch downy stay put.
- **One of yours out of its pen waits by the pen's gate** (`strayWait`): it works its way round the fence to the lane side and hops about within a couple of metres of the post, rather than wandering off into the meadow (the sixth playtest's sandbox day of trips left 15 of 19 loose). A hungry one still goes to food it can see.
- **Company:** downies of the same kind in a pen are a little happier (hunger rises 15% slower).
- **Down tufts:** drift down slowly and lie where they land until drawn up. Tufts left on the ground blow away overnight.
- **Thistle downies** hop over any fence that isn't high (a thistle behind a low fence brings the high fence line, see the lessons).
- **Active count:** keep at most about 60 simulated downies. Far-away wild ones can be despawned and respawned.

## Nettlebacks (the hazard)

Spiky, grumpy hedgehog-like creatures that roam the wilds at night and wander towards the ranch.
- They frazzle downies within 3 m (inside a pen, the downies run about within the fence) and eat down tufts lying on the ground.
- Puffing anything at one sends it rolling away squeaking. It flees and comes back later.
- A pen with a hedge upgrade keeps them out.
- **They show at night**: warm amber eyes lit from within, and a cool rim that comes up on them after dark (the fifth playtest saw a dark lump at 6 m). By frame readback (`ranch-pest-night.mjs`) at 11 pm, the brightest 1% over a pest 6 m off reads 189 of 255 against ground at 24 (10 m off, 189 against 26); with its night look off (the control), 32 and 72. **The glow carries**: a soft warm glow round the eyes, turned to the view, keeps about 15 CSS px across at any distance and fades in past 8 m (the sixth playtest's pest was a dark speck at 20 m). At 20 m up the ranch lane the brightest 1% reads 179 with 18.7 px lit (it was 114 and 1.3 px); its glow-off control reads as the old speck.
- **Hit, it scuffs a little dust**, small and faint (`Fx.scuff`): the building dust it used to raise hid the pest itself (71% of its box at night, 66% by day, after one Blow at 3.5 m; now 2.6% and 6%, by `ranch-blow-cloud.mjs` with an old-dust control).
- **Blow with nothing aboard still shoos one** (a puff of air, see the bellows).
- **A roaming one never sits in a corner**: brushing along a wall is no longer read as being blocked (a pest touching the valley's east wall took every step for a fresh detour along it, and slid into a corner), treats beyond the valley's walls are not treats, and one that has got under 1.5 m in 6 s goes somewhere else in the ranch, ignoring treats for a while (the sixth playtest's sat in the north-east corner at 35.6, 18.4 for over a game hour; the stuck watchdog acted only for one leaving or fleeing).

## The Exchange

A crate with a chalk price board by the house (on screen: the sell crate, with a "Sell fluff" plate). Blow fluff into it to sell it. It is built to be easy to hit from first person with the bellows:
- **Open to the lane:** a tall backboard leaning back and low flared sides, front open, so from the lane you see into it. Fluff that reaches the backboard drops in. (A funnel with a front board read as a closed lid and hid the inside.)
- **It draws fluff in:** fluff within 2.4 m of its middle, flying or lying on the ground, is pulled over the rim (`cratePull` in `sim.ts`). A blow that would land short or a metre wide still sells, and so does fluff shed nearby. Fluff further off is left alone.
- **The crosshair says when a blow would sell:** with fluff chosen, `wouldSell` flies the fluff ahead with the real step, pull and collisions, and the crosshair turns into a small gold funnel. Measured by `npx tsx scripts/ranch-sell-aim.ts` over 770 blows from 1.6 to 8 m with up to 1.2 m of aiming error: 99% sell (80% before the pull), and the mark matched the outcome every time.
- **It takes live downies for a live order** (and only then): a downy of yours **caught today** (or, for the rare order, the rare one) blown at it while today's order wants its kind is drawn in like fluff (twice as hard, lifted over the rim) and goes to the buyer, paying the order's price for it (`sellDowny`, `downyCratePull`, `wantsDowny`). One from your pens is refused, and says so as it lands by the crate: "The order wants one caught today." (the fifth playtest filled its live orders from its own pens, so the trip out could be skipped). The jar gives out the downy the order wants first (`nextDown`), so a fresh catch is never stuck behind one from a pen. With a downy chosen and such an order open, the crosshair shows the same funnel (`wouldSellDowny`), only for one it would take. By `ranch-sell-aim.ts`, 385 blown downies from 1.6 to 8 m with the same errors: 96% taken (100% to 5 m), and the mark matched every time. Without a live order the crate takes no downy.
- **Prices:** each kind of down has a base price that drifts daily within plus or minus 25%.
- **Saturation:** each tuft sold today takes 0.4% off that kind's price, never below 60% of the day's price, recovering overnight (`SATURATION`, `SAT_FLOOR`). There is no cap on a day's income: more downies make more coins. (It was 1.5% a sale with no floor, which capped a day of clover at about 533 coins however many downies you kept.)
- **Only your blows count for the lessons:** fluff the crate draws in from beside it (a downy that ate and shed nearby) still sells, but the sell lesson and the loop lesson wait for fluff you blew in.
- **Board:** shows today's prices with up and down arrows, and under them the day's orders.
- **Feedback:** coins pop out with a chime.
- **Daily orders, a choice each morning** (`offerOrders`, `postOrders`, `takeOrder`, `sellFluff`, `sellDowny` in `sim.ts`; measured by `npx tsx scripts/ranch-orders.ts`). The fourth playtest's top fun change: its board only asked for what the ranch already made (clover 18, then clover and ember, then ember and thistle, never the six brooks kept), there was nothing to choose and a missed order lost only its bonus: "after day 4 it's only chores". Now each morning (from dawn to noon; a later start waits for the next morning) **the board offers three orders and you take one**, at the board: a Board button (E or X) by it, one tag per offer ("20 clover fluff", "Pays 260 on top of sales"; "5 clover downies", "Caught today. Pays 330"; "Gold clover downy", "By the old oak. Pays 250, and stays"), Use says Take. The tags hang low, under the board's chalked prices (they covered them), and the board chalks each with what it pays ("pays 260"). The one taken is the day's order and the rest are wiped off (the first order, still open from the day before, stays); offers nobody takes go at dusk. They close at dusk (7 pm, `ORDER_BY`).
  - **A fluff order** for a kind you keep: about 70% of what those downies make by dusk (a meal every five hours or so, two fluff a meal; it was 45%, what you made anyway), at least 4 (a thistle 2). Its fluff sells at the board's price as usual and filling it pays its sum on top, set when it is posted so the tag can say it ("Pays 168 on top of sales": it read "Earns 168 more by dusk" beside a trip's "Earns 165 by dusk", and the fifth playtest read them as the same money; a trip's says "Caught today. Pays 165") (about the fluff's price again; a prized one twice that, "pays triple": a thistle's, and with Brook Hollow open every third day's first, a big one of nearly half as much fluff again). A day of tending at home: feeders kept up, fluff pulled.
  - **Live downies** of a kind you can catch today (clover; ember with the ridge open; brook with the hollow; never thistles, which come only at dusk): two to five, **caught today**, blown into the sell crate by dusk, each paying its price as it goes in (`LIVE_EACH`: clover 66, ember 110, brook 140; they were 55, 95 and 120). The count is set so the order is worth about a quarter more than the day's plain fluff order (`LIVE_OVER`; it was a tenth, and the fifth playtest found the trip extra work for the same coins), and the kind is one whose price fits that, with kinds you keep few of asked for more. A trip out instead of a day at home, so catching keeps its purpose once the pens are full: a careful catcher spends about 40 to 60 s on it, a careless one about twice that (ranch-catch's odds).
  - **The rare one, a hunt** (the fifth playtest's top fun change; the sixth's: "make the rare downy a real hunt"). ## Look and feel

Distinct from Sunup's tabletop diorama and Seedbound's almanac cards. It should feel like standing in a soft, windy pastel valley under a big sky.
- **Palette:** sage, cream, apricot, periwinkle and lilac, with long views fading into soft lilac haze.
- **Rendering:** painterly low-poly with smooth gradient shading. A gradient sky dome with drifting clouds, a sun and moon, and stars at night.
- **Grass:** instanced wind-swaying grass across the meadows. Seed fluff drifts in the air everywhere: that is the world's signature.
- **Downies:** soft and lit from behind (a fresnel rim) so they read against the grass. Squash and stretch on every hop. Simple, readable eyes.
- **Viewmodel:** the bellows sits lower right, brass and leather with a glass canister that shows the selected slot's contents. It bobs with walking and pumps when drawing; the drawn objects stream into the muzzle.
- **HUD:** minimal. The slot bar is bottom centre (icon, count, selected highlight). Coins and the time of day are top right. The crosshair changes when something drawable is targeted. Interact prompts appear near the centre and journal toasts at top centre. Keep the shell's top-left corner (60 px plus the safe area) clear.

## Performance

60 fps on a Pixel 8 Pro: Mali-G715, about 412 by 915 CSS px, DPR 2.6.
- Render at most DPR 2, and lower the render scale on `ctx.quality() === 'low'`.
- Aim for under 150 draw calls. Instance grass, clover patches, rocks and downies.
- Use one shadow-casting light with a small map, or blob shadows on low.
- No allocation per frame in hot paths. Guard every shader against NaN.

## Save

Save everything through `ctx.save(data, summary)` when the player sleeps, every 30 s, and on pause.
- **What to save:** the player's position and look, the canister, coins, the day and time, gates, pads and their upgrades, garden crops, silo contents, every downy (kind, position, hunger, which pen), tufts on the ground (optional), goals and upgrades.
- **Summary** for the title card, for example "Day 4, 3 pens, 1,240 coins".

## Priorities

Build in this order. Each step should be playable before the next.
1. **Ranch and Meadow:**
   - First-person movement and look on all three input kinds.
   - Bellows draw and puff with real physics.
   - Clover downies with hunger, eating and shedding.
   - Wild clover patches.
   - Pens and gardens on pads, the Exchange with prices.
   - Day and night, save and load, the HUD, the pause menu.
2. **The rest of the valley:**
   - Gates to Ember Ridge and Brook Hollow, ember, brook and thistle downies, nettlebacks.
   - The pen and garden upgrades, the silo, bellows upgrades, journal goals.
3. **Polish:**
   - Glide.
   - Juice everywhere: squash, puffs of fluff, coins, camera feel.
   - Ambient life: butterflies, drifting fluff, birds.

## As built: decisions and notes

- **Urgent lessons, in order of precedence:** slots, the order, live-order and rare-order lessons (which take over when the first order is posted, the first live order is taken, and the first rare one is taken), jump, running, dusk, night, the high fence, the pen limit, stray. An urgent lesson takes over a calm one and hands back; one urgent lesson never takes over another. The third playtest's crowded pen (13 in it) frazzled them all and the stray line nagged; the pen limit is now taught at eight, during the loop lesson if need be. A thistle that hops out of a low pen brings the fence line, not the stray line's nagging.
- **Lessons 1 to 9 run in order.** Lessons 10 to 16 come when they matter: dusk and night when the clock gets there (the night after the meadow, not after dusk, since staying up lets the dusk line lapse unlearned), **the feeder first, at its price (150) with a pen, as soon as the loop lesson is done**, before the garden and, with the first order taken, before dusk (the sixth playtest's garden lesson at 200 left 88 coins against a 300 feeder, and the first night woke the pen starving), then filling it straight after (the second playtest's journal jumped from buying it to the gate goal, and it sat empty for a day and a half while three downies wandered off; only food you put in counts, not a garden's crop tipping in by itself), the garden at 200 coins, the gates at 500 (only once you can pay), and the workbench at 400 once the feeder is filled and the ridge gate is open (the fifth playtest's jar lesson at 400 spent the gate's savings while the journal counted towards 500).
- **The stray line repeats** (`Lesson.repeat`): whenever a downy of yours hops out of its pen it takes the page over (like dusk), says so and points at it, and goes once it is back; it is never counted as learned. **With an order or hunt in hand it goes under the order's lines**, and the arrow stays on the hunt (off altogether while you are out of the ranch on a trip): the sixth playtest's hunt lost its step to "Pull in the downy that got out." with the arrow on the meadow. The sim marks such a downy (`strayFrom`) and emits `stray`; a downy coming down in a pen emits `penned`.
- **The porch downy's hunger waits** (held at 0.3 or below through the walk, pull and blow lessons) until the feeding lesson, which gives it an appetite (0.62); while the feeding and selling lessons lean on it, it never goes past 0.8, so it is hungry, never starving, when you come back with clover heads.
- **Two lessons take over the one on screen when they become ready, then hand back.** Slots comes when two kinds are first aboard (after Puff); jump comes when you step into the meadow, or push against something for more than a second (after the pen). Dusk and night are urgent too, and dusk lapses quietly at nightfall if you stay up.
- **A lesson already done when it comes up passes quietly**, with no line. On keys and pads Space and A always jump (nothing on screen depends on it), so the jump lesson usually passes quietly there.
- **Selling comes before the pen** (the brief had them the other way). The fed downy's down is lying right there to sell, coins arrive with that first sale counting up from before it, and you never spend coins you cannot see.
- **The clock holds at half past four in the afternoon** until the loop lesson is done (it was the meadow lesson: the fifth playtest's loop then ran from 16:30 through the pests to 2:40 am, and the sleep line lapsed both nights), so the first evening never lands in the middle of the basics, and the first order comes with the afternoon still to fill it. **Until the meadow is done, so does the hunger of every downy of yours** (penned, loose or on the porch: `Sim.clockHeld`, the `appetite` feature); through the loop lesson hunger runs, so its downies get hungry and are fed, and until the day runs on none of yours goes past 0.8. The second playtest penned its first downy at 0.36 and found it starving at 180 s, frazzled at 300 s and over the fence at 330 s while the journal still said to catch one in the meadow. Nettlebacks stay away until the night lesson.
- **Other tags hang quietly when they matter** (no line): the high fence after the first night or a thistle, a trough once a brook downy is caught, a down chute after 20 down sold, garden upgrades after planting, the silo when the canister is full or refuses a kind, a fifth slot once four kinds are aboard, glide vanes once a gate is open.

Save and returning players: lessons and their counts persist in the save (`learn`, `learnCount`). A save from before the lessons is credited with what it has clearly done (looked and walked, drawn, puffed, fed, sold, a pen, a penned downy, switched slots, a day gone by, a second garden or a planting, upgrades, gates), along with every lesson those come after, so a returning player is not taught again. `&taught` in the URL teaches everything (a sandbox, and what the single-feature checks use).

The journal page is never blank while there is something to do: the lesson while one is up (**an open order's tally kept under its line**, calm lines without a tick of their own); with no lesson up, **the day's orders with their tally** ("Ember fluff, 2 of 6.", `orderLines`; once all are filled, "Orders filled. New ones come at dawn." ticked) and the next journal goal under them, calm lines in the same hand. **The thistle goal has a clue** (it had none): by day "Thistles visit the ridge top at dusk.", from half past five to half past eight "Catch a thistle on the ridge top." with an arrow over the ridge top (on the nearest thistle once you are up there). **A gate goal counts the coins towards it** ("Open the Ember Ridge gate, 288 of 500.", `goalText`) and an arrow hangs over the gate (`goalSpot`), with the edge chevron for its first 6 seconds only, so the page is not a dead end while you save up; the big total counts what you have earned ("Earn 10,000 coins in all, 2,300 so far.") (a playtest found the page blank on day 2 at 197 coins, waiting for the garden lesson at 200, and gone for good after the gates). A goal just done (or an order filled) shows struck under "Earned 40 coins" for a few seconds, then the next goal takes the page; if a lesson has the page, it waits until the lesson has ticked and gone (for up to 6 s; older than that, it is dropped). The pause page's journal shows the goals done, in the order you did them, and the one you are on, last (`journalGoals`), not the ones to come (the third playtest's showed all eleven on day 1, the thistle too; the fourth's showed goal 6 unstruck between struck goals, as the ridge gate came before the first order). J puts the goal line away until the next goal or lesson. The pause page's "Now:" line shows the lesson, or else the goal. First-kind notes and the gate page wait while a lesson has the page.

Fixes found while measuring the lessons:
- **A touch in the gap between the stick and the look side** (or above the stick) landed on the canvas; Chrome's compatibility mousedown then asked for pointer lock, and after that every touch read as 0,0, so tags could not be tapped. Touch-born mouse events are now ignored, and the view takes touch taps itself.
- **Food puffed onto a slope used to skid on** (14 m in the sim test), because each dip counted as airborne and lost its ground friction. Rolling things now hug the ground. Feeding a downy went from 15 to 30 s to about 2 s.
- **A hungry downy that has spotted food now hurries to it.** It still bounces slowly while it is only hungry.
- **The feed goal counts only your own downies.** Wild ones foraging used to tick it.

**Keys.** E uses whatever is in reach; with nothing in reach it moves to the next slot that holds something (the table gives E to both). Q is the previous slot that holds something (empty windows are skipped); the wheel and 1 to 5 also work; F also draws, for trackpads. Esc or P pauses. J brings up the next goal when no lesson has the page. With no pointer lock (or `&nolock`), right-drag looks.

**Touch.** Tapping the bellows' body flicks to the next slot that holds something; tapping one of its windows picks that slot, and holding it for a little over half a second (a cream ring closes round it) tips it out. Under touch the windows spread at least 48 px apart on screen (`Viewmodel.resize`); on a tall phone they sit in two rows of two on the gauge plate (a fifth above), between the stick and the Blow button and left of the bellows' middle so every window target keeps at least 8 px from Blow (it was 2 to 3; now 11), the grips moved over to the right so they never cross a window. **The chosen window wears a cream ring on every device**, and each window carries a small icon of what it holds (a downy's face, a clover head, a sweetroot, dewberries or a wisp of fluff) wherever the windows are big enough (10 px across or more); they used to be plain coloured discs with a barely brighter rim. Each window's tap target is 45 px on the Pixel (it was 22), and none overlaps another or a button. A press that lands on a window and then moves is handed to the stick (or the look area), so a thumb that meant to walk walks and tips nothing out. On a tall phone the bellows shrinks and tucks in low, and the Pull, Blow and Jump buttons sit beside and above it. A quick tap anywhere that is not a button counts as a tap on what is under it (tags, windows).

**The puff of air** at the muzzle is a few small, faint wisps that thin out in a quarter of a second: measured on the Pixel's viewport, a blow hides at most 9% of the aim area (120 px round the crosshair), never a fifth (it was 61%, over a fifth for 0.35 s).

**Waking** after sleep turns you to face the yard (the crate and the pens) at the darkest moment of a shorter fade (1.25 s), not the front door you used.

**The workbench** can be used from 3.5 m, and its tags hang just in front of it turned to face you wherever you stand (they used to hang edge-on along the house wall).

**The gates' boards** carry the way they open and its price, painted like a tag (the ridge gate's was a blank board), lit from within at night: by frame readback the plank reads 126 with ink at 29 at 11 pm (day 119 and 28; with its night light off, 12 and 1, which is how the third playtest found it). **Every hanging tag is lit the same way now** (`TagMenu.setNight`), and so are the painted signs (the ranch gate's arch and teaching sign, the meadow's signpost, the crate's and the bench's plates): by `ranch-tags.mjs`, a pen post's tag at 9:40 pm reads 129 with ink at 16 (day 109 and 13; the fourth playtest measured 3 and 1, and the check's glow-off control reads 3 and 0), the sleep tag at 11:55 pm 168 and 16 (playtest 11 and 1), the signpost at 11 pm 159 against its daytime 145, the gate sign 163 against 150. The sleep tag's note says "Saves the ranch" (it read "Day 1, saves the ranch"). On a tall phone the zone's name comes up below the journal page, not behind it.

**The canister.** One slot per kind: a full slot refuses more of its kind, and a full canister refuses a new kind (the "full" cue and a short note under the crosshair, eight words or fewer, which once windows are known says how to make room: "No window free. Hold one to empty it." or "Window full. Hold one to empty it." on touch, "Hold Q to empty one." on keys, "Hold LB to empty one." on a pad; `fullNote`).

**The count by the crosshair** sits on a soft ink backing (62% alpha, 7 px either side), so it never runs into world words ("Sell²fluff", "by du14sk", a downy's face). **With the sell mark up it moves off the crate's sign** (`Hud.keepCountOff`: above the crosshair, or above or below the sign): from the lane at 2 to 6 m it covered the sign on 17 to 21 of 90 sell aims ("Sell fl2ff", the sixth playtest), now on none.

**The desktop journal page** sits 18 px above the bottom edge, rotation and all, with one to three lines (the sixth playtest's ran 13 px off it at 1280 by 800).

**Pointer lock refused.** A browser that refuses pointer lock (headless, a frame that forbids it) falls back to right-drag after the first refusal: the look line says "Right-drag to look around.", the "Click to look around" hint goes and clicks stop asking (they asked on every click, and the line stayed "Click the view to look around."). A refusal within 1.5 s of letting go of the lock (Esc) is only the browser's short wait and changes nothing.

**Pens.** Food and down in flight slip between the rails, so you can puff food into a pen at the floor; rolling on the ground, the fence stops them. Downies need about 1.05 m to clear a low fence: calm hops are 0.4 to 0.6 m, a crowded or neglected one about 1.3 m, thistles 1.6 m. A thistle in a low pen backs off from the rails and hops so the top of its hop is over them. The high fence is 2 m.

**Blowing a downy into a pen** used to fall short: aimed at the pen floor from the lane, it met the fence below its top. Now a pen helps one blown at it from outside (`penPull`, like the sell crate's pull on fluff): within 3.2 m of the fence and heading in, it is steered towards the middle and lifted over the rail, and once inside it settles rather than sailing over the far side; one blown while you lean on the fence leaves the bellows above the rail. A blown downy remembers where you stood, so one blown inside a pen is left alone. With a downy chosen the crosshair shows a little fence when a blow would land it in a pen (`wouldPen`, which flies it ahead with the real step). Measured by `npx tsx scripts/ranch-sell-aim.ts` over 125 blows from 1 to 8 m back from the fence, across it and up to 1.8 m off its middle: 97% land in the pen (10% without the help), and the mark matched every time.

**Food groups.** Each group has one food (clover heads, sweetroot, dewberries), so clover, ember and brook downies only eat their own group, always their favourite (2 down). Thistles eat anything for 2 down. A trough gives brook downies a third.

**Wild downies** get hungry at half the rate and never past 0.7, and forage the wild food (their meals make no fluff). Groups refill one at a time, out of sight (over 30 m away); thistles come to the ridge top from half past five to half past eight in the evening and leave after dark if not caught. Wild spawning stops at 56 downies.

**Nettlebacks** come in from the north edge of the meadow after half past eight at night (two at first, up to four), head for the ranch gate, then wander between pens without hedges, eating down on the ground and frightening downies within 3 m (penned ones run about inside the fence and stop shedding). A hedge keeps them out and keeps its pen calm. Anything puffed, in the air or skidding fast, sends one rolling, and so does a puff of air from an empty Blow within 5 m; it flees for 20 s and comes back. At dawn, or when you sleep, they leave; after a sleep the next night's first comes a few seconds after dark.

**Gardens** grow 2 (3 with rich soil) every 1.5 game hours (1 with a sprinkler), and stop when 12 are lying on the bed, or, beside a pen with a feeder, tip the crop into that feeder instead. "Pull the crop" empties a bed so something new can be puffed in; this is how the planting goal is met with the starter garden. A bare garden alone keeps a pen of six fed (20 a day against about 16 eaten); one upgrade keeps it fed with food to spare, both keep a full pen of ten. Every round of tending now finds food: by `ranch-balance.ts`, the gardens grew nothing in 0 or 1 of 27 rounds per ranch (the third playtest: 4 of 9).
