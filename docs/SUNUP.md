# Sunup: design

Sunup is the homestead life sim. It runs on the farm simulation in `src/sim` under one fixed set of rules (the Sunup preset), drawn by the 3D diorama renderer. The sections below describe the simulation as first built with its switchable axes; only the Sunup values apply now, and the other axes stay in the simulation only because its tests exercise them.

Three farming games share one farm. The player can change any rule at any moment and keep playing the same farm. The point is to find out which farming game is the most fun by playing them, and the mixes between them.

## Sunup as it plays now

What follows nine playtests: Spring 1 to Autumn 1 on a phone, then 31 days on a phone and a desktop, then 33 days to Winter 3 on a phone and a desktop, then 24 days on a phone and a desktop pass, then 22 days on a phone with real touch and a desktop pass, twice, then 24 days on a phone and 6 on a desktop, then Spring 1 to Autumn 2 on a phone and 5 days on a desktop, then Spring 1 to Autumn 3 on a phone and Spring 1 to 7 on a desktop (with a 6-farm, 30-day bot run each time). These rules hold only under Sunup's rules (`homestead(s)` in `src/sim/actions.ts`); the other rule sets keep the older behaviour their tests check.

**The day.** 6:00 to midnight in 60 real seconds on one field, 75 on two, 90 on three, 105 on four and 120 on five or six (`SUNUP_DAY_SECONDS`, `daySeconds` in `src/sim/actions.ts`; it was 240, then 150, then 120 for every farm, and the fourth playtest found the last job done between 8 and 11 most days, after 15 to 40 seconds of work). A new save holds the clock at 6:00 until the farmer first does something (`held`), so reading the first line costs no daylight. A crow pecks for 15 real seconds before it takes a crop, whatever the day's length (`peckMinutes` in `src/sim/step.ts`). Stamina is 100: water 2 a plot (it was 1, and a one-field farm never dipped below 75), harvest, plant and pick off bugs 1 each, hoe and build 2, clearing a withered crop nothing; the upgraded hoe charges 2 for each plot past the first, the upgraded can half a watering for each (rounded up: a row of four costs 4), and a picking for each crop it takes bugs off. A dry day's watering on one field takes about a third of the day's stamina; round 8: on two fields, with the copper can that comes with the second, about a third too (it was two thirds: "30 plots cost 60 of 100 stamina"), so sprinklers pay their way from the third field. Sleeping before 6 pm always asks first ("Sleep now?", `SLEEP_ASK_BEFORE`; round 7: Space on a Sleep button that kept the keyboard put the farmer to bed at 3:27 pm, twice), never while the first lesson teaches it or with no stamina left for any job (`sleepAsk` in `src/sim/actions.ts`). At any hour it asks while bugs (with the stamina to pick them), a pecking crow or a flock day would cost crops tonight, or while a request still wants seed planted today, on its last day for it (`owedToday` in `src/sim/goals.ts`; round 7: one plant short of "Plant 9 today", the tester slept without a word and lost it). The question is the hour and the one thing most at stake, eleven words at most (`sleepText` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`; it ran 18 words over three sentences): "It is 3:27 pm.", "It is 9:00 pm. A request needs 1 more planted today.", "It is 5:46 pm. Bugs left on crops spread tonight." (round 6: a farm slept early lost crops the tester never saw go). Day 3 is dry (the rain comes on day 4), so the seeds sown on day 2 want water.

**Soil and water.** The can wets a plot for one night; rain (or a sprinkler) soaks it for two, one in a heat wave. The can waters crops, not bare soil: aimed at bare soil it says "Nothing growing here" and the act button stays dim and wordless. A crop left dry on a heat wave's night withers (`HEAT_DRY`), so a heat wave means watering everything that day; the line "Heat wave. Water every crop today." points at a thirsty crop while one is left. The can's water is always on the can: a level up the side of the tool on the rack, and a gauge on the act button while it is in hand. Empty, both say Empty, and the line "Can empty. Fill the can at the well." points at the well. Round 8: the copper can and hoe work the aimed plot's whole row of its field, the gold ones that row and the one beside it (below it, or above on the field's last row), never the path or the next field (they worked three plots of a row and a three by three square: a four-wide row still took two taps, and the gold square covered no more of a field in four taps than the copper rows); the reach marks show only the plots they will really work (`area` in `src/sim/actions.ts`). The can picks the bugs off every crop it waters, and off the plot it was aimed at, in the same tap (the eighth playtest's can picked the bugs instead of watering, and on a heat wave four bugged crops were left dry unsaid).

**The watering chore, cut** (round 8; the tester's single biggest fun change: "The daily loop is 30 identical watering taps and two walks to the well"; 16 plots took 14.5 s, two fields overflowed the day while spring was slack). The second field comes with the copper can, however it was bought (`buyParcel` in `src/sim/step.ts`; a save with two fields gets it on load): its card says so ("A copper can comes with it: it waters a whole row."), and so does its toast. A second well stands where the paths between the first four fields cross (`PROPS` in `src/sim/map.ts`, at 6,9), a step from each of them and in view from the first field on a phone (the yard well sat at the screen's right edge); the well lines point at it from the fields (from the yard, at the yard well). A tap on the ground beside a well is the well; a tap on a plot is always the plot, even beside the field well and even while the can is empty (an empty can turned a plot tap into a well trip), and a plot whose ground shows behind the well's drawing takes its own tap. The smith's gold can (two rows) wants reputation 4 and 1,200 (it wanted 8 and 1,500), something to buy in the middle of the game (the tester sat on 1,590 coins by Summer 9 with nothing worth buying). Two fields of thirsty crops are now eight taps and about a third of the stamina; `scripts/balance.ts` measures the rest (Balance, round 8).

**A pair is a trade read at a glance** (round 9; the tester's single biggest fun change: "It is calm and works, but it is a sum, not a choice. By mid-summer I was taking the bigger number and sleeping by noon."). Each offer headlines what it clears in all, its pay less its seed (`clearOf` in `src/sim/goals.ts`), then the plots and days it ties up: "+885 / for 10 plots, 7 days" beside "+1,045 / for 12 plots, 6 days" (`noteText` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`; "+415 over market" put the bigger number on the bold side nearly every time, and on Summer 4 the sunflowers won on every line). The two notes hang side by side on every screen, the same lines in the same places (what it clears, plots and days, what and for whom, "Plant today, seed 405", "Due Summer 6, no day to spare", and while the season's fair still wants requests, "In time for the fair" or "After the fair"). Neither side clears as much on as few plots in as few days (`dominates`, `tradeOf`): one is quicker or smaller, the other bigger. Only crops planted after Accept count for a request (`Order.from`, set at Accept; the tester's "18 radishes, 16 planted" were market radishes sown before Accept), so an offer never says "planted". An offer that no longer fits the farm by the time the board is read (seed sown since took its soil, coins went on a field) is made again to fit it, its own sort and crops first (`freshenOffers`, run by the `freshen` command as the Requests tab renders), so no side is ever stamped "No room" or "Need 60". A taken note says the same figure ("For Fen and daughters, +1,000 in all"). The clash line counts the coin box's coins and says what is ripe beside them ("Both need 705 of seed. You have 255, plus 265 ripe."; it said "You have 520" with 255 in the box). The crate hands a request over the moment its last crop goes in, by day (`deliverReady`), with its toast and a chip over the plot ("All 8 radishes in"): no Deliver stamp to find, one clear way (the board's foot says "The crate ships a request when it is all in.").

**The season's fair** (round 9; "No reason to keep going after about day 16"). Each season ends with a fair on its last day that wants so many requests filled that season: two more than the fields owned when the season began, one fewer in summer, when only two crops a request can ask for grow, and one more in autumn, when five can (`fairWant`, `FAIR_MORE`, `FAIR_LEAN` in `src/sim/constants.ts`). The first Accept sets it (with fewer than six days of the season left, the next season's); each new season sets the next (`startFair`). Every request filled in its season counts, in full or short (`payRequest`). On the fair's night (`judgeFair`, in `nightfall`) a farm that got what it wanted wins a ribbon, which hangs on the house's front beside the door (`src/render3d/ribbons.ts`), and a fifth off the next field (`fieldPrice`, `WorldState.fieldOff`; one prize waits at a time); the morning card says so first ("The fair gave you a ribbon", or "No ribbon at the fair, 2 of 4 requests"). It is taught in one line once the first request is planted, on a day nothing else new came ("The fair wants 4 requests by Spring 10."), which brings one quiet line under the stamina bar on the day card ("Fair Spring 10: 1 of 4"; `fairShort` in `src/ui/hud.ts`); the board says it in two notes ("Fair on Spring 10: 1 of 4 requests filled." and "Its prize: a ribbon and a fifth off a field."). The quick, small side of a pair counts as much as the big one, so near the fair the trade is real. The fifth and sixth fields cost 3,000 and 4,500 (they were 2,400 and 3,500), so the coins have somewhere to go and the prize matters. A careful bot that takes the side due by the fair while it still wants requests (`botPick` with `fair`, now the default) wins 73 to 77% of fairs over 40 days (Balance).

**The first fifteen minutes** (round 9: engaging 3 of 10; Spring 4 and 6 had no jobs, the first real choice came on Spring 9, about eight minutes in). The board's first offer is a small pair of cheap crops, three to five plots each (`FIRST_PAIR_MOST`; it was three radishes, taken at once), so the first choice comes on Spring 3. Two requests can be open before the first is filled (`requestSlots`), so a second pair comes the morning after the first is taken. The field lesson waits only for the first pair to be decided (it waited for the first request's delivery, and Spring 5 to 8 sat on 490 coins with a full field). New requests say so on the morning card ("two new requests on the board"); the eighth playtest's came with only a badge. A paid lesson (the scarecrow, the sprinkler) waits while paying for it would leave less than the requests want for seed (`seedWanted` in `src/games/homestead/lessons.ts`; the tester's scarecrow lesson took 40 of 120 coins the morning an offer needed 60, and a neighbour left seed money three mornings running), and the scarecrow lesson waits for a bare plot in the middle of a field (with field 2's middle full it built at a corner). `scripts/qa/sunup-first15.mjs` plays the tester's first fifteen minutes (Spring 1 to Summer 6) on a phone and counts the choices and the dead days (Balance).

**Seasons.** The day card gives the season's length ("Spring 7 of 10"; round 7: nothing on screen said it, and nine lettuces planted at 6 pm were lost at the turn). In the last three days of a season the seed picker also offers the next season's crops, marked with their season and days ("Summer, 6 days"; round 9: "Summer crop" hid the days). Crops that would still be green at the turn and do not grow in the next season are marked "Too late": a tap on one says why in the picker itself ("Carrots won’t ripen before summer."), the picker stays open and the seed in hand stays (round 7: the tap closed the picker with no word the tester saw, and the next eleven plot taps planted nothing; `seedTap` in `src/ui/hud.ts`). A crop that ripens only if watered every night until the turn is marked "Just in time", and choosing it says so in the picker ("Lettuces ripen just before summer. Water every night."; `justInTime` in `src/sim/actions.ts`). Two nights before the turn the morning card says how many green crops will not ripen in time ("14 green crops won’t ripen before autumn"), and the caption on each says "won’t ripen before autumn" (`doomedAtTurn`). At the turn, ripe crops stay to be picked; what the turn takes is simply gone. A crop that withers (by drought, bugs, heat, or on the night of the turn) stays on its plot one day, clears for free, and is gone the night after. If a farm has too few coins for any seed that can go in, nothing ripe to pick and nothing in the crate, and bare soil (or nothing growing at all), a neighbour leaves enough for four packets of the cheapest, or for its bare plots when fewer (`rescue` in `src/sim/day.ts`; round 8: it came only with nothing growing, and the tester sat on 10 coins and 7 bare plots for three summer days). The new season is told on the morning card ("Summer begins."), not in a toast of its own behind it.

**The crate pays at once** (round 7; the tester's biggest fun change). Every crop is paid for at market the moment it goes into the crate (`payAtCrate` in `src/sim/actions.ts`): the coin box counts it at once, a small rise of coins shows over the crate as the crop lands (a quick run of picks adds up on one), and a harvest can be planted again the same day. The seventh playtest had 0 coins and 10 to 22 bare plots from Summer 1 to 5, and on Summer 5 picked eleven crops and still could not plant. A crop sown for a request is paid at market too, and counts in the crate for the request; the request pays its gain over market when it is filled. The morning card still sums yesterday's sales (round 9: "Yesterday you sold 8 for 340", as the crate already paid); what the crate holds back overnight is nothing now, so "kept for a request" is gone from it.

**Requests** are the morning's decision (`offerRequests` in `src/sim/goals.ts`). Round 9: the first is a small pair, pinned when the board is read (it was three radishes, taken at once); two can be open until the first is filled. After that a morning with no offer waiting brings a pair of offers, and the player takes one with the board's Accept (`acceptRequest`; the `accept` command); the other goes at once, with a word ("Old Tamsin will find carrots elsewhere."). Planting never takes a request: the fifth playtest's market planting took one nobody had read and let its partner go. An offer is never filled from the crate by accident (its crops sell at market), and the crate keeps crops only for requests the player took.

The fifth playtest found the round 4 pairs identical (net pay per plot-day within 3% in all five; "corn 675 over 42 against sunflower 675 over 42"), so the choice was made by what fit. Now the two of a pair differ in kind (`CONTRASTS`, `RequestKind`), a bold one against a safe one, and the morning after offers a different sort:

- **risk:** a rush (sow today, or the day the soil comes free, and due the day it ripens: no day to spare) against an easy one (sow by tomorrow, up to two days to spare); one crop is enough for this sort (summer has two a request can name);
- **time:** a long crop against a quick one at least two days faster (radish against carrot, say);
- **size:** many plots of a cheap crop against a few of one with seed half as dear again.

Round 8: the two of a pair are priced on what each is worth for each plot-day of soil it holds, its pay less its seed over its plots and its crop's days (`worthRate`, `pricePair` in `src/sim/goals.ts`; the eighth playtest's pumpkins came to about 19 a plot-day against 12.5 for the lettuces beside them, because pumpkins are worth more at market, and the choice was a sum). The safe one is worth the two crops' average market rate (`marketRate`, what a crop fetches over its seed by its days) plus `REQUEST_RATE` (5) a plot-day, the bold one a little more for its risk, days or plots (`REQUEST_EDGE`: a rush 15%, a long or big one 10%), so the two stay within a fifth of each other (`REQUEST_CLOSE`) and the choice turns on risk, timing and plots; each still gains at least 15% over market. And a pair offers only what the farm can afford to start now: its coins and what is ripe to pick today, after what taken requests still owe (round 8: 255 coins were offered seed of 480 or 270); the board's clash line counts the same coins. (Before round 8 the safe one gained 5 over market a plot-day and the bold one was priced to match it on a soil value, `neutralValue`, with at least 1.2 times its gain.) The bold one must keep the slack its sort promises, or the pair is not offered. So the bold one is meant for a farm with free soil and coins and a player who will tend it, the safe one for a full or poor farm or a busy player (the play-outs under Balance show the bold pick better in 63% of calls with soil free above the median against 54% below, and in 47% of a diligent player's calls against 36% of a busy one's). Each still:

- is all fresh sowing (round 6): it counts only crops sown after it was pinned, each for one request, never a crop already growing or in the crate (the sixth playtest was paid 240 over market for ten lettuces already growing, "nothing to sow", and 380 for corn sitting in the crate). Seed sown for a request the player took is marked as its own (`claimCrops`, `Crop.for`; a crop's id is larger than the request's, as both come from one counter), the request due first choosing first; seed sown after an offer was pinned counts for it once it is accepted; a crop sown for it and picked waits in the crate for it (`Order.got`), and only those fill it (`canDeliver`, `wanted`); other crops of the kind sell at market. It prefers a crop the farm is not growing, for variety, but even one it grows asks for all of its count sown fresh; never a crop that regrows; a crop already asked for is asked again only once the request for it is fully sown;
- competes with the other of its pair (round 6): the farm could not sow both, for want of free soil or coins by their sow-by days (`competes`); the two grow together, up to three times, until they cannot both be sown, and a pair that cannot be made to compete is offered only when no pair can (on mornings like the tester's, one pair in seven was a real choice);
- fits what the farm can raise by its sow-by day (`sowBudget`: coins, the crate, what ripens the day before) and the soil free by then, asks for no more than 60% of the farm's plots (`REQUEST_MOST`), and gains at most 14 coins a plot owned (`gainCap`);
- is sown by a day or two from now (near a season's end sooner, so it keeps its days to spare; when the soil is full, by the day enough comes free, up to six days on) and is due its crop's growing time after that and its days to spare, never after its season ends.

Round 7: a pair is always two different crops (four pairs in seven had one crop on both sides, "10 corn +440 or +350", one offered twice with the same numbers), never the same crops as the offer before (`offerKey`, `WorldState.lastOffer`), and always competes: together the two want more soil or seed coins than the farm has free by their planting days, after what taken requests still owe (`competes`; the tester was offered two that each wanted 450 coins and 10 plots from a farm with 1,180 coins and 22 bare plots). A pair that cannot be made to compete is no longer offered; the morning brings one request instead, or none. Over the cap (`gainCap`) the bold one is held to it and the safe one priced to match, so the counts that make the two compete stay; each still gains at least 15% over market (`REQUEST_FLOOR`), and a request asks for up to 32 plots (`REQUEST_MAX`; 24 could never overflow a farm of three fields). The board says what makes the pair a choice, under it, in eight words or fewer: "Both need 26 plots. You have 22." or "Both need 900 of seed. You have 600." (`clashOf`, `clashWords`). Seed and soil a taken request still owes are spoken for, both when an offer is priced and on its Accept stamp (`owed`; round 7: a pair wanted 450 of seed from a farm with 270 and 180 owed, and was withdrawn at dawn); a crop ripe today counts towards the seed money (it pays at the crate whenever it is picked), so an offer priced on it is not withdrawn at dawn while it is still on the farm (`sowBudget`).

Round 7: one loss no longer voids a request. A taken request can be planted, and replanted after a loss, until the last day seed sown would still ripen by its due day (`lastSowOf`): its days to spare are days to replant ("Plant 1 more by tomorrow"). One that can no longer be met in full but has crops growing for it is short: its note says so at once ("14 of 15 can come, pays 285"), a toast after a loss says what it means for the request ("A crow took a sunflower. 14 of 15 can come."; `requestAfterLoss`), and once nothing more is growing for it (or on its day) it is paid the same share of its gain for each crop it gets, and (round 8) the same share of its reputation point, which counts towards the next (`partPay`, `isShort`, `addRep`, `WorldState.repPart`; the board's Deliver hands it over by day). Round 8: a short fill earned no reputation, and crows that took one crop from each of five requests kept the copper can locked at reputation 2; now 9 of 10 earns nine tenths of a point, the board says it in quarters ("Reputation 2¾"), and a toast by day says "+¾ reputation". A request left with nothing coming and no time to plant again is lost there and then, by day, with its cause in a toast ("Old Tamsin gave up on the carrots (crows ate 1)") and on the next morning card (`dropLost`; a pumpkin request that could no longer be met still read as live). The card names a cause once: "Crows ate 1, Pell’s pickles found sunflowers elsewhere" (it added "(crows ate 1)" again).

Each morning a request that can no longer be met is withdrawn (`canMeet`, `withdrawRequests`): one the player took says so on the morning card, with its cause (round 6: "Old Tamsin found lettuces elsewhere" said nothing of the crows that ate them). A crop sown for a request that is lost (to crows by day or night, bugs, a dry or heat-wave night, or the turn) is kept on the request (`lostFor`, `Order.lost`), and the card names the cause that took most ("Old Tamsin found lettuces elsewhere (crows ate 2)", "gave up on the corn (bugs killed 3)"), or, with no loss, why it could not be met ("too few sown in time", "no room to sow them", "no coins for the seed", "not picked in time"); the taken note says so as it happens ("2 growing, 1 lost to crows, wanted by Spring 9"). An offer nobody took goes quietly, so the card never names a request the player did not choose or never saw. The first request (three radishes) is fresh sowing too: after the board is read, a line says to sow for it ("Plant 3 radishes for the request."). A pair comes only while fewer than four are open. Round 8: what is on offer comes first on the board, the pair and its clash line at the top, then the pinned morning card, the reputation and the requests already taken (on a phone the clash line sat below the fold, under the requests taken); with nothing on offer, its foot says "A request pays its gain once filled." (it said "The crate keeps what requests need", which every crop paying at the crate contradicted). The board hangs a pair together with "or" between the two notes, and each offer headlines its gain over market ("+195 over market"; "Pays 1,370" was mostly market value for carrots already growing), then what it wants and for whom ("6 corn for Mabel’s bakery"), what to sow and by when ("Sow 6 today, seed 270"), and when it is ripe and due with its days to spare ("Ripe in 7 days, due Summer 7, no day to spare"), with an Accept stamp (dashed "Need 270" or "No room" when it cannot be met now; `noteText` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`). A taken note says what is growing for it and in the crate ("4 growing, 2 in the crate, wanted by Spring 9"; it said "0 of 9 in the crate" while nine grew for it). The Board button's badge counts only new notes, pinned since the board was last read (`readBoard`; it counted every open note). Round 7: the board keeps the last morning card pinned among its requests, to read again (it showed for a few seconds and was gone; round 8: it is read again as the farm is now, so "4 ripe to pick" goes once they are picked, `pinnedNews` in `src/ui/hud.ts`); its notes scroll inside it with a fade at the foot while there is more below (five notes ran past the foot of a phone's board and read as cut off), and `scripts/qa/overlap.mjs` scrolls a full board with a real thumb to its last stamp. The board's notes are short: "Reputation 2. One more for a copper can." (the smith only once his lesson has come; before it, "Reputation 0. Each request filled adds one."; round 7: every tool still to come counts, so reputation 3 with no copper tools is "Three more for a gold hoe.", not "The smith will make anything."), "Accept one of a pair." The smith wants reputation 2 and 6 for the hoes, 3 and 4 for the cans (round 5 eased the gold ones from 8 and 10; round 8 the gold can from 8 and 1,500 to 4 and 1,200, and the copper can comes with the second field), and the board names only tools the farm has. The words are "reputation" throughout.

**Fields** cost 250, 800, 1,500, 3,000 and 4,500 (round 9: the last two were 2,400 and 3,500; a fair's ribbon takes a fifth off the next), and from the third the land agent also wants reputation 2, 3, 5 and 7 (`FIELD_REP`; it was 2, 4, 6 and 8, and the fifth playtest sat on 1,755 coins by Summer 9 with nothing to buy). The field lesson waits until buying the second field leaves 150 coins for seed (`FIELD_SEED`; the tester spent 250 of 270 on its word and could not plant it for five days), and a field's card says what buying it leaves when that is under 150 ("That leaves 70 for seed."). The price shows once on each card: on the stamp ("Buy for 250", "Need 800"), or, when the stamp says "Reputation 4", in the card's words ("Four by four, meadow, 1,500 coins"). Saves keep their fields; fields still for sale take the new prices on load.

**Aiming and tapping.** A faint frame always marks the plot in front; it brightens, with the verb's badge, where acting would do something. When the plot in front has nothing to do, acting snaps to the nearest useful plot in reach; a repeat from a held act key never snaps to a plot behind the farmer (holding Space while walking sowed a 45-coin strawberry behind the farmer), and says nothing where there is nothing to do. The act button says its verb where acting would do something; elsewhere it is dim and wears the tool in hand. Tapping a plot walks there and does the job; taps made while the farmer is busy wait their turn and are done in the order tapped (up to 48; one more is refused with a word, never dropped silently; pushing the stick drops them). A tap takes the tool for the job when the one in hand cannot do it and a free job waits there: the hands for a ripe crop, bugs, a crow or a withered crop, the can for a thirsty one (never seeds or the hoe, which spend coins or turn meadow). A crow comes before water: a tap with the can on its crop takes the hands. Round 8: a tap with the can on a dry, bugged crop picks the bugs and waters it in one tap (round 4 picked the bugs instead, and on the eighth playtest's heat wave four bugged crops were left dry; `tapTool`).

**The tap queue** (round 8; four bugs, fixed at the root in `queueTap`, `doTap` and `tapJob` in `src/sim/actors.ts`, and tested as a whole by a property test over random tap sequences in `tests/homestead-round8.test.ts`):

- Each tap keeps the tool and the seed it was tapped with (`Tap` in `src/sim/types.ts`), and the walk to it keeps them too (`Actor.aim.tool`, `.seed`): picking another seed or tool while taps wait changes nothing they do (Carrot picked, four plots tapped, then the 1 key for Radish planted `2:radish 3:radish 4:radish 5:carrot`; on the phone 8 of 12 corn went in as "Just in time" sunflowers, and the turn killed 9 crops). The hand takes a tap's tool as the tap is made (the can for a thirsty crop), never later when its turn comes, under a player who has picked another tool since.
- Only a true duplicate is merged: the same tile, tool and seed as the tap just before it (or the walk under way), with nothing between, and never a trip to a well (two well trips in one queue kept only the first; the can ran dry and on a heat day the crops after it withered, on four days). A well tap with the can still full says so, rather than walking there for nothing.
- A crow or bugs go to the front of the queue, after any other crow or bugs already waiting, and even before the walk under way to a calmer job, which waits its turn after them; the same job waiting further back moves up with it (a crow tapped during a 24-tap watering pass waited at the back; one needed three taps 30 game minutes apart, and crows ate five request crops).
- Seeds on a plot not yet free pick its ripe crop (one picked once) or clear its withered one first, then plant there, in one tap (harvest a row, pick seeds, tap the same row: the seed taps became duplicate harvest taps and 7 of 10 plots stayed bare).
- The lesson pointer never asks a question for a tap made with the seeds or the can in hand, nor while the farmer is busy (a tap then waits its turn): such a tap is the plot's own job (`pointerTap` in `src/games/homestead/pointer.ts`; the fourth of seven sunflower taps landed on the scarecrow lesson's tile, "Build a scarecrow?" opened, and the six taps after it went on the question, twice). With the hands in hand and nothing waiting, a tap on the target still asks, and a question that opens leaves the queue as it was.

`scripts/qa/sunup-queue.mjs` checks all of it on a phone with real touch (on the round 7 code all eight of its checks fail).

A tap on the ground beside the well, or on the well's drawing, walks there and fills the can; a tap on the notice board's drawing reads it. The lesson pointer's chevron, over a target in view, takes no taps of its own: it is drawn half a tile up, over the plot to the north, and in the fifth playtest a tap on that plot counted as a tap on the pointer, took the Build tool without a word, and every tap after built a scarecrow (five of them, 220 coins down to 20). Now only a tap inside the target tile counts (`pointerTap` in `src/games/homestead/pointer.ts`); the arrow at the screen's edge, when the target is out of view, is the pointer itself and walks there. For the scarecrow and sprinkler lessons such a tap asks first ("Build a scarecrow?", its cost, what it does, the coins in hand; "Build for 40" or "Not now"), and yes builds one there, once, through the `buildAt` command, with the tool in hand left alone; with too few coins it says so ("A scarecrow costs 40. ## The lessons now

One line at a time, eight words or fewer, in the words on the buttons. Round 9: a lesson whose job the farmer has already done passes quietly, never shown, its features still brought in (`pass`), and a count line goes the moment its count is met (`instant`). Lines about a place point at it: a cream chevron just over the top of the thing (aimed half a tile up), or, while it is out of view, an arrow on a dark disc at the screen's edge, turned towards it. The arrow takes a tap; the chevron lets taps through to the plot under it, and a tap inside its target tile is the pointer's (see Aiming and tapping).

What a lesson brings onto the screen (a tool, the board, the board's Fields, Seeds and Smith tabs, the forecast, the stamina bar, the View button) arrives with its line, the first moment the line can be seen: not while a reminder speaks over it, nor under the board. A lesson counts as done only once its line has been on screen (`Tutor.update` in `src/games/homestead/lessons.ts` counts the seconds it was visible: not while asleep, nor under the board or a picker). Round 7: every line stays on screen at least four seconds (`MIN_SHOW`), its job done or not: done early, it keeps its words and drops its pointer until then (but never lingers into the next morning). Lessons end on an action, or once read while the farmer stands idle, never on a timer alone: a passive line counts only the seconds it was seen with the farmer idle (`idleFarmer`), and ends sooner when its act is done (the seventh playtest never saw the turn, bugs, forecast or sprinkler lines, yet all four were marked learned; the forecast and sprinkler lines ended on 8 and 10 s timers). A calm lesson ends when the farmer has done what it says after seeing it. An urgent one ends when the farmer dealt with the trouble, or when the trouble went away after the line had been up 1.5 seconds; one whose trouble went away unseen is not done and waits for the next time. Urgent lessons have an order: the crow over the bugs, and those over the new field; the one taken over from comes back after. Lessons that bring something new onto the screen come one a day, each with its own line: the crow (its line only), the Build tool the next morning, the forecast the morning after, the sprinkler on a later day once the farm has two fields and the coins for one, and the smith on a day when nothing else came and he can make something the farm can use and pay for (`stagger`). A passive line seen on its day goes with it (the forecast line lingered two days). A lesson with a `hold` steps aside, unfinished, while it holds, and comes back: the scarecrow line waits until a scarecrow can be paid for (the fifth playtest was told "Scarecrows cost 40. ## Crows and bugs

Crows and bugs are let onto the farm quietly from day 5, once delivering requests is known. Until the crow lesson is done, one crow comes on a dry day with something growing, between 6:30 and 8:00, while the farmer is surely out, and a crow still pecking at nightfall takes nothing: the first crop a crow takes is one the farmer watched it go for. The crow line takes over from any other line (the bug line included), names the crop, and points at the crow. It ends when the crow is shooed, or when it has gone after its line was seen; the scarecrow line and the Build tool come the next morning. After the lesson, on a dry morning, a crow comes for most fields with a growing crop no scarecrow watches (`CROW_PER_FIELD`). Sunup's scarecrow watches the five by five square around it (`SUNUP_SCARECROW_RADIUS`; it was seven by seven, and in the third playtest two of them ended the crows for good), but only the plots right beside it for sure (`SUNUP_SCARECROW_SURE`, `guardedSure`): the outer ring of its square is its edge. One in the middle four plots of a field watches all of it, its far row and column only at the edge, where a crow still tries now and then (`CROW_EDGE`, less often when fewer of the field's crops are at an edge, `CROW_EDGE_FULL`); it lands only on an edge plot, never beside a scarecrow, and goes for an unwatched crop first. One at a field's edge or corner leaves the far side open. Covering a field for sure takes a scarecrow on each of its middle four plots, so crows stay a small daily chore, not a solved one. Some days demand attention (round 5: one crow in 22 days after the lesson, and sleeping early cost nothing): on a dry morning after the scarecrow lesson, crows are about with a chance of 22% (`FLOCK_CHANCE`), told on the morning card; one more crow comes for each field with a growing crop no scarecrow watches for sure, between 11:00 and 20:00 (`FLOCK_HOURS`), and the crows still to come when the farmer goes to bed take a crop each, so the sleep question asks at any hour that day ("Crows are about: those still to come take a crop each."). Bugs land on a few growing crops each morning, and again at 13:00 (`MIDDAY_MINUTE`, `landBugs`), so the afternoon has a look worth taking (`BUG_CHANCE`); left on a crop, they spread overnight to every growing neighbour of the same kind and to about half of the others (`BUG_SPREAD`), and a crop left bugged for three nights withers. Round 6: losses are told. A crop a crow takes by day counts on the morning card with the night's ("crows ate 2"; `eatenToday`; it counted only the night's), with a toast when it goes; the card says how far bugs spread overnight ("bugs spread to 5 crops"; `DayReport.spread`); and the sleep question asks at any hour while bugs or a crow are on the farm. `tests/homestead-pressure.test.ts`, `tests/homestead-crow.test.ts` and `scripts/qa/lessons.mjs` check all of it.

**Rain days** (round 6: four of the tester's first ten days had 6 to 10 seconds of work) are planting weather: rain waters what goes in today for two nights, and the morning card says so ("what you plant today needs no water", round 7: only with bare soil and the coins for a seed; it came with 0 coins and every plot sown); a wet morning's pair tries the rush first (sow today). With requests all fresh sowing, a wet day has a sowing to plan.

## The three games

**Sunup: a day on the homestead.** 3D, close camera, walk the farmer, real-time day with stamina, classic water-every-day growth, a shop, wild weather, solo, a kit of tools. The fun is the routine: wake, walk the rows, water and pull, toss the crop in the crate, chase off a crow, sleep. Each day is a small run you get better at.

**Patchwork: the almanac puzzle.** 2D, wide camera, point at tiles, twelve moves a day with time frozen, synergy soil (moisture, nutrients, companions, quality), seasonal almanac goals with stars and a seed draft, wild weather with a three-day forecast, solo, one smart action. The fun is planning: where does the corn go so the beans can climb it and the lettuce gets its shade, with rain due on Thursday.

**Bushel & Co.: the farm that runs itself.** 3D, wide camera, point and drag, a continuous clock you can speed up, classic growth, town orders with deadlines and a market that moves, calm weather, a crew of farmhands, one smart action. The fun is the machine: hire hands, put down sprinklers, lay out fields to fill the orders, then speed up time and watch it work.

## Axes

Every axis is independent. A mix that matches no preset is a custom game and is just as valid.

| --- | --- | --- |
| View | 2D, 3D | Which renderer draws. Nothing in the rules. |
| Camera | Close, Wide | Close follows the farmer. Wide frames the farm with pan and zoom. |
| Control | Walk, Point | Walk: move with keys, stick or a thumb pad, act on the tile in front. Point: tap or drag tiles to queue jobs and the farmer walks to them. |

## Shared rules

- The farm is 20 by 15 tiles. Six fields of 4 by 4. The first is owned and tilled; the rest are bought from the notice board. A new field starts as meadow and needs tilling once. Tilled soil stays tilled.
- Eight crops. Seeds are paid for when planted. Harvests go to stock, then to the crate.
- Classic growth: a crop grows one day each night it was watered that day. Three dry nights in a row and it withers. Ripe crops wait and never wither.
- Synergy growth: a crop grows if moisture at nightfall meets its thirst (1, lettuce 2). Moisture drops by one each night, two in a heat wave. Quality from 0 to 3 is shown while it grows: +1 a companion next door, +1 a sunflower next door (bees), +1 rich soil (nutrient 2 or more), minus 1 spent soil (0), minus 1 crowded by two or more of the same crop, minus 1 if it ever went thirsty. Quality multiplies the price by 1, 1.25, 1.5 or 2. Harvests use nutrient; beans put it back on their tile and the four around it; empty soil recovers slowly.
- Rain waters every plot. Sprinklers water the 3 by 3 around them each morning (Sunup's stands up on a stone footing, a brass riser with a blue-capped spinning head; the old low pad with a pipe along the soil read as a dropped tool; they cost 200; Sunup's water for ten mornings, a season, then wear out, with a word on the morning card and "N mornings left" on the caption, `SPRINKLER_MORNINGS`: two 120-coin sprinklers had ended watering for good). Scarecrows keep crows off everything within three tiles (Sunup's within two: a five by five square, sure only beside it).
- Crows land on unprotected crops. Walk near one or tap it to scare it. Left alone, it eats the crop after two game hours (turns: at nightfall).
- Bugs appear on a few growing crops each wild morning and spread to the same crop next door (in Sunup to every growing neighbour of the same kind and half of the others). Bugged crops stop growing and die after four nights. Tend to pick them off.

## Switching

A switch never loses progress, moves the farmer into a wall, or strands a job.

- Day to Turns: moves left = the smaller share of time or stamina left. Turns to Day: the clock jumps to match moves used, stamina matches moves left. Flow keeps the clock where it is.
- Classic to Synergy: watered plots read as moist. Synergy to Classic: moist plots read as watered today.
- Crew off: hands go into the bunkhouse, their jobs go back on the board, no wages. Crew on: the first time, one hand joins for free.
- Wild to Calm: crows fly off and bugs go.
- Point to Walk: the farmer drops queued jobs (hands keep theirs under Crew).
- Almanac starts at the current season the first time it is switched on, with every crop already in the ground unlocked.
