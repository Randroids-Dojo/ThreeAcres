# Sunup: design

Sunup is the homestead life sim. It runs on the farm simulation in `src/sim` under one fixed set of rules (the Sunup preset), drawn by the 3D diorama renderer. The sections below describe the simulation as first built with its switchable axes; only the Sunup values apply now, and the other axes stay in the simulation only because its tests exercise them.

Three farming games share one farm. The player can change any rule at any moment and keep playing the same farm. The point is to find out which farming game is the most fun by playing them, and the mixes between them.

## Sunup as it plays now

What follows seven playtests: Spring 1 to Autumn 1 on a phone, then 31 days on a phone and a desktop, then 33 days to Winter 3 on a phone and a desktop, then 24 days on a phone and a desktop pass, then 22 days on a phone with real touch and a desktop pass, twice, then 24 days on a phone and 6 on a desktop (with a 6-farm, 30-day bot run each time). These rules hold only under Sunup's rules (`homestead(s)` in `src/sim/actions.ts`); the other rule sets keep the older behaviour their tests check.

**The day.** 6:00 to midnight in 60 real seconds on one field, 75 on two, 90 on three, 105 on four and 120 on five or six (`SUNUP_DAY_SECONDS`, `daySeconds` in `src/sim/actions.ts`; it was 240, then 150, then 120 for every farm, and the fourth playtest found the last job done between 8 and 11 most days, after 15 to 40 seconds of work). A new save holds the clock at 6:00 until the farmer first does something (`held`), so reading the first line costs no daylight. A crow pecks for 15 real seconds before it takes a crop, whatever the day's length (`peckMinutes` in `src/sim/step.ts`). Stamina is 100: water 2 a plot (it was 1, and a one-field farm never dipped below 75), harvest, plant and pick off bugs 1 each, hoe and build 2, clearing a withered crop nothing; the upgraded can and hoe charge 1 for each plot past the first. A dry day's watering on one field takes about a third of the day's stamina, on two fields about two thirds, so sprinklers and the copper can pay their way. Sleeping before 6 pm always asks first ("Sleep now?", `SLEEP_ASK_BEFORE`; round 7: Space on a Sleep button that kept the keyboard put the farmer to bed at 3:27 pm, twice), never while the first lesson teaches it or with no stamina left for any job (`sleepAsk` in `src/sim/actions.ts`). At any hour it asks while bugs (with the stamina to pick them), a pecking crow or a flock day would cost crops tonight, or while a request still wants seed planted today, on its last day for it (`owedToday` in `src/sim/goals.ts`; round 7: one plant short of "Plant 9 today", the tester slept without a word and lost it). The question is the hour and the one thing most at stake, eleven words at most (`sleepText` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`; it ran 18 words over three sentences): "It is 3:27 pm.", "It is 9:00 pm. A request needs 1 more planted today.", "It is 5:46 pm. Bugs left on crops spread tonight." (round 6: a farm slept early lost crops the tester never saw go). Day 3 is dry (the rain comes on day 4), so the seeds sown on day 2 want water.

**Soil and water.** The can wets a plot for one night; rain (or a sprinkler) soaks it for two, one in a heat wave. The can waters crops, not bare soil: aimed at bare soil it says "Nothing growing here" and the act button stays dim and wordless. A crop left dry on a heat wave's night withers (`HEAT_DRY`), so a heat wave means watering everything that day; the line "Heat wave. Water every crop today." points at a thirsty crop while one is left. The can's water is always on the can: a level up the side of the tool on the rack, and a gauge on the act button while it is in hand. Empty, both say Empty, and the line "Can empty. Fill the can at the well." points at the well. The copper can and hoe work three plots along the field's row, the aimed plot in the middle, slid along so all three stay in its field (never the path or the next field); the gold ones a three by three square; the reach marks show only the plots they will really work (`area` in `src/sim/actions.ts`).

**Seasons.** The day card gives the season's length ("Spring 7 of 10"; round 7: nothing on screen said it, and nine lettuces planted at 6 pm were lost at the turn). In the last three days of a season the seed picker also offers the next season's crops, marked "Summer crop" (and so on). Crops that would still be green at the turn and do not grow in the next season are marked "Too late": a tap on one says why in the picker itself ("Carrots won’t ripen before summer."), the picker stays open and the seed in hand stays (round 7: the tap closed the picker with no word the tester saw, and the next eleven plot taps planted nothing; `seedTap` in `src/ui/hud.ts`). A crop that ripens only if watered every night until the turn is marked "Just in time", and choosing it says so in the picker ("Lettuces ripen just before summer. Water every night."; `justInTime` in `src/sim/actions.ts`). Two nights before the turn the morning card says how many green crops will not ripen in time ("14 green crops won’t ripen before autumn"), and the caption on each says "won’t ripen before autumn" (`doomedAtTurn`). At the turn, ripe crops stay to be picked; what the turn takes is simply gone. A crop that withers (by drought, bugs, heat, or on the night of the turn) stays on its plot one day, clears for free, and is gone the night after. If a farm ever has nothing growing, nothing in the crate and too few coins for any seed, a neighbour leaves enough for four packets of the cheapest. The new season is told on the morning card ("Summer begins."), not in a toast of its own behind it.

**The crate pays at once** (round 7; the tester's biggest fun change). Every crop is paid for at market the moment it goes into the crate (`payAtCrate` in `src/sim/actions.ts`): the coin box counts it at once, a small rise of coins shows over the crate as the crop lands (a quick run of picks adds up on one), and a harvest can be planted again the same day. The seventh playtest had 0 coins and 10 to 22 bare plots from Summer 1 to 5, and on Summer 5 picked eleven crops and still could not plant. A crop sown for a request is paid at market too, and counts in the crate for the request; the request pays its gain over market when it is filled. The morning card still sums yesterday's sales ("sold 8 for 340"); what the crate holds back overnight is nothing now, so "kept for a request" is gone from it.

**Requests** are the morning's decision (`offerRequests` in `src/sim/goals.ts`). The first is three radishes, pinned when the board is read and taken at once, and nothing more comes until it is filled. After that a morning with no offer waiting brings a pair of offers, and the player takes one with the board's Accept (`acceptRequest`; the `accept` command); the other goes at once, with a word ("Old Tamsin will find carrots elsewhere."). Planting never takes a request: the fifth playtest's market planting took one nobody had read and let its partner go. An offer is never filled from the crate by accident (its crops sell at market), and the crate keeps crops only for requests the player took.

The fifth playtest found the round 4 pairs identical (net pay per plot-day within 3% in all five; "corn 675 over 42 against sunflower 675 over 42"), so the choice was made by what fit. Now the two of a pair differ in kind (`CONTRASTS`, `RequestKind`), a bold one against a safe one, and the morning after offers a different sort:

- **risk:** a rush (sow today, or the day the soil comes free, and due the day it ripens: no day to spare) against an easy one (sow by tomorrow, up to two days to spare); one crop is enough for this sort (summer has two a request can name);
- **time:** a long crop against a quick one at least two days faster (radish against carrot, say);
- **size:** many plots of a cheap crop against a few of one with seed half as dear again.

The safe one gains `REQUEST_RATE` (5; it was 6 before round 6, when a request could count crops already growing) coins over market for each plot and day its sowing takes; the bold one is priced so the two come out alike for a typical farm (`neutralValue`): its gain, a request with no day to spare counted at four in five (`REQUEST_RISK`), less its soil, a plot-day worth 3 coins for rush and easy, 5 for long and quick (a plot held days longer ties up coins and soil that would have been sown again) and 2 for big and small (`REQUEST_SOIL`; tuned on the play-outs below, where one value of 3 for all left the quick one better in 71% of the calls that were not a wash, and the big one in 69%). The bold one must offer at least 1.2 times the safe one's gain (`REQUEST_BOLD`) and keep the slack its sort promises, or the pair is not offered. So the bold one is meant for a farm with free soil and coins and a player who will tend it, the safe one for a full or poor farm or a busy player (the play-outs under Balance show the bold pick better in 63% of calls with soil free above the median against 54% below, and in 47% of a diligent player's calls against 36% of a busy one's). Each still:

- is all fresh sowing (round 6): it counts only crops sown after it was pinned, each for one request, never a crop already growing or in the crate (the sixth playtest was paid 240 over market for ten lettuces already growing, "nothing to sow", and 380 for corn sitting in the crate). Seed sown for a request the player took is marked as its own (`claimCrops`, `Crop.for`; a crop's id is larger than the request's, as both come from one counter), the request due first choosing first; seed sown after an offer was pinned counts for it once it is accepted; a crop sown for it and picked waits in the crate for it (`Order.got`), and only those fill it (`canDeliver`, `wanted`); other crops of the kind sell at market. It prefers a crop the farm is not growing, for variety, but even one it grows asks for all of its count sown fresh; never a crop that regrows; a crop already asked for is asked again only once the request for it is fully sown;
- competes with the other of its pair (round 6): the farm could not sow both, for want of free soil or coins by their sow-by days (`competes`); the two grow together, up to three times, until they cannot both be sown, and a pair that cannot be made to compete is offered only when no pair can (on mornings like the tester's, one pair in seven was a real choice);
- fits what the farm can raise by its sow-by day (`sowBudget`: coins, the crate, what ripens the day before) and the soil free by then, asks for no more than 60% of the farm's plots (`REQUEST_MOST`), and gains at most 14 coins a plot owned (`gainCap`);
- is sown by a day or two from now (near a season's end sooner, so it keeps its days to spare; when the soil is full, by the day enough comes free, up to six days on) and is due its crop's growing time after that and its days to spare, never after its season ends.

Round 7: a pair is always two different crops (four pairs in seven had one crop on both sides, "10 corn +440 or +350", one offered twice with the same numbers), never the same crops as the offer before (`offerKey`, `WorldState.lastOffer`), and always competes: together the two want more soil or seed coins than the farm has free by their planting days, after what taken requests still owe (`competes`; the tester was offered two that each wanted 450 coins and 10 plots from a farm with 1,180 coins and 22 bare plots). A pair that cannot be made to compete is no longer offered; the morning brings one request instead, or none. Over the cap (`gainCap`) the bold one is held to it and the safe one priced to match, so the counts that make the two compete stay; each still gains at least 15% over market (`REQUEST_FLOOR`), and a request asks for up to 32 plots (`REQUEST_MAX`; 24 could never overflow a farm of three fields). The board says what makes the pair a choice, under it, in eight words or fewer: "Both need 26 plots. You have 22." or "Both need 900 of seed. You have 600." (`clashOf`, `clashWords`). Seed and soil a taken request still owes are spoken for, both when an offer is priced and on its Accept stamp (`owed`; round 7: a pair wanted 450 of seed from a farm with 270 and 180 owed, and was withdrawn at dawn); a crop ripe today counts towards the seed money (it pays at the crate whenever it is picked), so an offer priced on it is not withdrawn at dawn while it is still on the farm (`sowBudget`).

Round 7: one loss no longer voids a request. A taken request can be planted, and replanted after a loss, until the last day seed sown would still ripen by its due day (`lastSowOf`): its days to spare are days to replant ("Plant 1 more by tomorrow"). One that can no longer be met in full but has crops growing for it is short: its note says so at once ("14 of 15 can come, pays 285"), a toast after a loss says what it means for the request ("A crow took a sunflower. 14 of 15 can come."; `requestAfterLoss`), and once nothing more is growing for it (or on its day) it is paid the same share of its gain for each crop it gets, without the reputation point (`partPay`, `isShort`; the board's Deliver hands it over by day). A request left with nothing coming and no time to plant again is lost there and then, by day, with its cause in a toast ("Old Tamsin gave up on the carrots (crows ate 1)") and on the next morning card (`dropLost`; a pumpkin request that could no longer be met still read as live). The card names a cause once: "Crows ate 1, Pell’s pickles found sunflowers elsewhere" (it added "(crows ate 1)" again).

Each morning a request that can no longer be met is withdrawn (`canMeet`, `withdrawRequests`): one the player took says so on the morning card, with its cause (round 6: "Old Tamsin found lettuces elsewhere" said nothing of the crows that ate them). A crop sown for a request that is lost (to crows by day or night, bugs, a dry or heat-wave night, or the turn) is kept on the request (`lostFor`, `Order.lost`), and the card names the cause that took most ("Old Tamsin found lettuces elsewhere (crows ate 2)", "gave up on the corn (bugs killed 3)"), or, with no loss, why it could not be met ("too few sown in time", "no room to sow them", "no coins for the seed", "not picked in time"); the taken note says so as it happens ("2 growing, 1 lost to crows, wanted by Spring 9"). An offer nobody took goes quietly, so the card never names a request the player did not choose or never saw. The first request (three radishes) is fresh sowing too: after the board is read, a line says to sow for it ("Plant 3 radishes for the request."). A pair comes only while fewer than four are open. The board hangs a pair together with "or" between the two notes, and each offer headlines its gain over market ("+195 over market"; "Pays 1,370" was mostly market value for carrots already growing), then what it wants and for whom ("6 corn for Mabel’s bakery"), what to sow and by when ("Sow 6 today, seed 270"), and when it is ripe and due with its days to spare ("Ripe in 7 days, due Summer 7, no day to spare"), with an Accept stamp (dashed "Need 270" or "No room" when it cannot be met now; `noteText` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`). A taken note says what is growing for it and in the crate ("4 growing, 2 in the crate, wanted by Spring 9"; it said "0 of 9 in the crate" while nine grew for it). The Board button's badge counts only new notes, pinned since the board was last read (`readBoard`; it counted every open note). Round 7: the board keeps the last morning card pinned at the top of its requests, to read again (it showed for a few seconds and was gone); its notes scroll inside it with a fade at the foot while there is more below (five notes ran past the foot of a phone's board and read as cut off), and `scripts/qa/overlap.mjs` scrolls a full board with a real thumb to its last stamp. The board's notes are short: "Reputation 2. One more for a copper can." (the smith only once his lesson has come; before it, "Reputation 0. Each request filled adds one."; round 7: every tool still to come counts, so reputation 3 with no copper tools is "Three more for a gold hoe.", not "The smith will make anything."), "Accept one of a pair." The smith wants reputation 2 and 6 for the hoes, 3 and 8 for the cans (round 5 eased the gold ones from 8 and 10), and the board names only tools the farm has. The words are "reputation" throughout.

**Fields** cost 250, 800, 1,500, 2,400 and 3,500, and from the third the land agent also wants reputation 2, 3, 5 and 7 (`FIELD_REP`; it was 2, 4, 6 and 8, and the fifth playtest sat on 1,755 coins by Summer 9 with nothing to buy). The field lesson waits until buying the second field leaves 150 coins for seed (`FIELD_SEED`; the tester spent 250 of 270 on its word and could not plant it for five days), and a field's card says what buying it leaves when that is under 150 ("That leaves 70 for seed."). The price shows once on each card: on the stamp ("Buy for 250", "Need 800"), or, when the stamp says "Reputation 4", in the card's words ("Four by four, meadow, 1,500 coins"). Saves keep their fields; fields still for sale take the new prices on load.

**Aiming and tapping.** A faint frame always marks the plot in front; it brightens, with the verb's badge, where acting would do something. When the plot in front has nothing to do, acting snaps to the nearest useful plot in reach; a repeat from a held act key never snaps to a plot behind the farmer (holding Space while walking sowed a 45-coin strawberry behind the farmer), and says nothing where there is nothing to do. The act button says its verb where acting would do something; elsewhere it is dim and wears the tool in hand. Tapping a plot walks there and does the job; taps made while the farmer is busy wait their turn and are done in the order tapped (up to 48; one more is refused with a word, never dropped silently; pushing the stick drops them). A tap takes the tool for the job when the one in hand cannot do it and a free job waits there: the hands for a ripe crop, bugs, a crow or a withered crop, the can for a thirsty one (never seeds or the hoe, which spend coins or turn meadow). Bugs and a crow come before water: a tap with the can on a dry, bugged crop picks the bugs off (watering it left them on, and a bugged crop does not grow; `tapTool`). A tap on the ground beside the well, or on the well's drawing, walks there and fills the can; a tap on the notice board's drawing reads it. The lesson pointer's chevron, over a target in view, takes no taps of its own: it is drawn half a tile up, over the plot to the north, and in the fifth playtest a tap on that plot counted as a tap on the pointer, took the Build tool without a word, and every tap after built a scarecrow (five of them, 220 coins down to 20). Now only a tap inside the target tile counts (`pointerTap` in `src/games/homestead/pointer.ts`); the arrow at the screen's edge, when the target is out of view, is the pointer itself and walks there. For the scarecrow and sprinkler lessons such a tap asks first ("Build a scarecrow?", its cost, what it does, the coins in hand; "Build for 40" or "Not now"), and yes builds one there, once, through the `buildAt` command, with the tool in hand left alone; with too few coins it says so ("A scarecrow costs 40. You have 0.") and nothing else happens. The build goes with the lesson's own target only (round 6: with no bare soil the scarecrow lesson's pointer fell back to a bug and kept the build, so a tap on the bugged crop asked "Build a scarecrow?" and "Build for 40" built nothing); the build lessons wait for bare soil anyway. A picker belongs to its tool: when a tap takes another tool (the can for a thirsty crop) the seed or build picker closes for good (`pickersAfter` in `src/ui/hud.ts`), so the lesson pointer, which an open picker hides, comes back (it stayed hidden until Escape, which a phone has not). A tap with the Build tool already in hand is the player's own build. A tap on a seed that cannot go in ("Too late") says why in the open picker and keeps the seed in hand (round 7; see Seasons). Walking with the act button held does quick jobs on the move, a little slower, instead of stopping at every plot.

**The keyboard.** Every button shows its key while a mouse or keyboard is in use: the tools 1 to 5, Enter on Sleep, B on the board, C on the view button, and a number on each card in the seed and build pickers. While a picker is open the arrow keys choose in it (they never walk the farmer; WASD still do), a number picks that card, and Enter or Escape closes it; Escape never reaches the game switcher while a picker is open. B puts the keyboard on the board (on its open tab), and while a card is open Tab and Shift+Tab go round its own buttons only, never the HUD behind it; Escape closes it and gives the keyboard back to the farm. A button reached with Tab takes Enter itself; a button clicked with the mouse or tapped gives the keyboard straight back to the farm. Round 7: a HUD button over the farm never keeps the keyboard from it: Space and E act on the farm unless a card is open, and any key that walks or acts (WASD, the arrows, E, Space, Q, B, C, the tool numbers) takes the focus off the button first (`FARM_KEYS` in `src/game/input.ts`; the seventh playtest's Tab left the keyboard on Sleep, WASD walked on, and Space put the farmer to bed at 3:27 pm, twice). On an open card Space still presses the card's button. `scripts/qa/sunup-keys.mjs` checks it with a real keyboard and mouse. The View button (top right, on every device once its lesson has come) switches between close up and the whole farm, like C.

**The cameras.** A tall phone screen looks down at 70 degrees and fits the farm's whole depth between the day card and the tool rack, about ten tiles across. Across the west side (the first field, the path beside it, the second field, and the well's front) it holds still on the middle of the first two fields, easing a fifth of a tile east towards the well, so the first field's left column stays in view and takes a tap (round 5: from the second field's east side or the well it had slid off screen); further east it slides on with the farmer, and it always keeps the farmer well inside the screen (`tallX` in `src/render3d/camera.ts`, `tests/homestead-camera.test.ts`). Close up, a phone sees about two fields, so standing in the second field hides the first field's left column (a bugged crop there showed only as an edge arrow) and a trip to the well hides half the first field: a tap on View shows the whole farm, every plot of all six fields between the day card and the controls (`scripts/qa/lessons.mjs`, `scripts/qa/touch.mjs`), sitting in the middle of the band between them rather than the middle of the screen (round 6: the top 40% was blank sky; `WIDE_TALL_BAND`), and a second tap comes back. A wide screen's close camera looks 1.4 tiles south of the farmer and leans towards what a lesson points at, so the rack and the lesson line never cover the crops being taught (`scripts/qa/overlap.mjs` measures it); round 7: from up to 12 tiles away and by up to four tiles, so from the house the well is on screen when the line sends the farmer there (it sat at x 1,287 on a 1,280 px screen; now about 1,187). Rain and storms darken the day but much less at first light: a 6 am storm reads as well as a sunny 6 am (`scripts/qa/lessons.mjs`). Round 7: at night worked soil keeps a cool moonlit sheen (`uSoilNight` in `src/render3d/materials.ts`, more under rain and storm clouds), so a hoed field reads at 10 pm in a storm (frame luma 44 of 255 against 21 for the meadow beside it; it was 15, darker than the meadow, phone-095).

**The board and the HUD.** Every tool on the rack wears its name, not only the one in hand, and the Build tool's hammer stands upright, so it never reads as the Hoe's slanting blade beside it; the Board and View buttons wear their words, and the board's close button says Close. Stamps are sentence case; what you cannot afford is a dashed "Need 400". A field or a smith's tool asks before it buys, with its price once. One field bought makes one toast. The morning card is one sentence with a capital at its start only ("Old Tamsin paid 145, sold 8 for 340, 6 ripe to pick."), of three clauses at most ("Summer begins." counts as one): the most pressing are kept (what was lost, a request that went, what was paid and sold, what the turn will take, what is ripe, what the crate keeps) and said in reading order (`morningNews`). It says how many crops are ripe to pick ("2 grew" counted crops that changed their look, when all 27 had aged a day). On the last two mornings of a season what the turn will take comes first of all ("4 green crops won’t ripen before summer"; the fifth playtest's full cards left it out, and the turn took seven beans unannounced), and a flock day says "crows are about today". Town names read as names inside it ("for the lighthouse keeper"). Harvest words over a plot are short ("2 of 3 radishes", "3 radishes ready") and wrap rather than run across the screen, and words over a plot at the screen's edge ("Already watered") are kept whole on it. They sit on a paper chip, ink on cream, so they read on dark soil (contrast over 7), and stay up nearly two seconds (round 6: cream on brown soil was nearly invisible); a request's count follows only crops sown for it. A crop a crow takes by day says so ("A crow took a carrot."). A note's or caption's phrases never break across lines ("no / day to spare", "4 days to / go"; `keepWhole` in `src/ui/dialogs.ts`). A request handed over by day says who took the crops ("+145 coins, +1 reputation"). The forecast wears a word under each day's picture (Sun, Cloud, Rain, Storm, Heat), in 11 px type; the seed cards' lines are 12 px. The weather's name keeps to one line ("Heat wave" wrapped the day card); on a phone the forecast drops its "then" so the top right never runs off the edge (round 6: on a heat-wave day the coins box and the View button ended at 418 px on a 412 px screen, the coins cut off). On a screen 900 by 640 or larger the HUD's type is a size up (date 23 px, tool and button words 13 px; it read tiny at 1280 by 800), the board is wider with notes in cards 300 px or wider at 15 px (they were 13 px in narrow cards on a mostly empty board), and the key caps are 12 px (about 8). Too tired for a job, the act button is dim and wordless over it (it stayed lit with Water), one refusal shows at a time, and the taps still waiting are dropped (two refusals drew over each other). In the seed picker, seed the purse cannot pay for is faded, edged with a dashed line and priced in red. The Build tool wears a hammer (it looked like a pencil). Seeds and sprouts are drawn a size up (they were dots on a phone), and a bugged crop wears a rust ring on its soil as well as yellowing.

## The lessons now

One line at a time, eight words or fewer, in the words on the buttons. Lines about a place point at it: a cream chevron just over the top of the thing (aimed half a tile up), or, while it is out of view, an arrow on a dark disc at the screen's edge, turned towards it. The arrow takes a tap; the chevron lets taps through to the plot under it, and a tap inside its target tile is the pointer's (see Aiming and tapping).

What a lesson brings onto the screen (a tool, the board, the board's Fields, Seeds and Smith tabs, the forecast, the stamina bar, the View button) arrives with its line, the first moment the line can be seen: not while a reminder speaks over it, nor under the board. A lesson counts as done only once its line has been on screen (`Tutor.update` in `src/games/homestead/lessons.ts` counts the seconds it was visible: not while asleep, nor under the board or a picker). Round 7: every line stays on screen at least four seconds (`MIN_SHOW`), its job done or not: done early, it keeps its words and drops its pointer until then (but never lingers into the next morning). Lessons end on an action, or once read while the farmer stands idle, never on a timer alone: a passive line counts only the seconds it was seen with the farmer idle (`idleFarmer`), and ends sooner when its act is done (the seventh playtest never saw the turn, bugs, forecast or sprinkler lines, yet all four were marked learned; the forecast and sprinkler lines ended on 8 and 10 s timers). A calm lesson ends when the farmer has done what it says after seeing it. An urgent one ends when the farmer dealt with the trouble, or when the trouble went away after the line had been up 1.5 seconds; one whose trouble went away unseen is not done and waits for the next time. Urgent lessons have an order: the crow over the bugs, and those over the new field; the one taken over from comes back after. Lessons that bring something new onto the screen come one a day, each with its own line: the crow (its line only), the Build tool the next morning, the forecast the morning after, the sprinkler on a later day once the farm has two fields and the coins for one, and the smith on a day when nothing else came and he can make something the farm can use and pay for (`stagger`). A passive line seen on its day goes with it (the forecast line lingered two days). A lesson with a `hold` steps aside, unfinished, while it holds, and comes back: the scarecrow line waits until a scarecrow can be paid for (the fifth playtest was told "Scarecrows cost 40. Sleep to sell the crate." at 6 am with a request waiting), the sprinkler line until a sprinkler can, and both, with the turn line, until there is bare soil, and point at it (round 6: "Build a scarecrow on bare soil." came with every plot full, and "Spring ends soon. Plant summer seeds." with the chevron on a bug). No line sends the farmer to bed with work waiting: "Crops grow overnight. Sleep." and "No coins left. Sleep to sell the crate." wait until nothing is ripe, thirsty, bugged or pecked at (and the second for something the night would really sell: with the crate paying at once, nothing does, so it no longer comes).

## Crows and bugs

Crows and bugs are let onto the farm quietly from day 5, once delivering requests is known. Until the crow lesson is done, one crow comes on a dry day with something growing, between 6:30 and 8:00, while the farmer is surely out, and a crow still pecking at nightfall takes nothing: the first crop a crow takes is one the farmer watched it go for. The crow line takes over from any other line (the bug line included), names the crop, and points at the crow. It ends when the crow is shooed, or when it has gone after its line was seen; the scarecrow line and the Build tool come the next morning. After the lesson, on a dry morning, a crow comes for most fields with a growing crop no scarecrow watches (`CROW_PER_FIELD`). Sunup's scarecrow watches the five by five square around it (`SUNUP_SCARECROW_RADIUS`; it was seven by seven, and in the third playtest two of them ended the crows for good), but only the plots right beside it for sure (`SUNUP_SCARECROW_SURE`, `guardedSure`): the outer ring of its square is its edge. One in the middle four plots of a field watches all of it, its far row and column only at the edge, where a crow still tries now and then (`CROW_EDGE`, less often when fewer of the field's crops are at an edge, `CROW_EDGE_FULL`); it lands only on an edge plot, never beside a scarecrow, and goes for an unwatched crop first. One at a field's edge or corner leaves the far side open. Covering a field for sure takes a scarecrow on each of its middle four plots, so crows stay a small daily chore, not a solved one. Some days demand attention (round 5: one crow in 22 days after the lesson, and sleeping early cost nothing): on a dry morning after the scarecrow lesson, crows are about with a chance of 22% (`FLOCK_CHANCE`), told on the morning card; one more crow comes for each field with a growing crop no scarecrow watches for sure, between 11:00 and 20:00 (`FLOCK_HOURS`), and the crows still to come when the farmer goes to bed take a crop each, so the sleep question asks at any hour that day ("Crows are about: those still to come take a crop each."). Bugs land on a few growing crops each morning, and again at 13:00 (`MIDDAY_MINUTE`, `landBugs`), so the afternoon has a look worth taking (`BUG_CHANCE`); left on a crop, they spread overnight to every growing neighbour of the same kind and to about half of the others (`BUG_SPREAD`), and a crop left bugged for three nights withers. Round 6: losses are told. A crop a crow takes by day counts on the morning card with the night's ("crows ate 2"; `eatenToday`; it counted only the night's), with a toast when it goes; the card says how far bugs spread overnight ("bugs spread to 5 crops"; `DayReport.spread`); and the sleep question asks at any hour while bugs or a crow are on the farm. `tests/homestead-pressure.test.ts`, `tests/homestead-crow.test.ts` and `scripts/qa/lessons.mjs` check all of it.

**Rain days** (round 6: four of the tester's first ten days had 6 to 10 seconds of work) are planting weather: rain waters what goes in today for two nights, and the morning card says so ("what you plant today needs no water", round 7: only with bare soil and the coins for a seed; it came with 0 coins and every plot sown); a wet morning's pair tries the rush first (sow today). With requests all fresh sowing, a wet day has a sowing to plan.

## The three games

**Sunup: a day on the homestead.** 3D, close camera, walk the farmer, real-time day with stamina, classic water-every-day growth, a shop, wild weather, solo, a kit of tools. The fun is the routine: wake, walk the rows, water and pull, toss the crop in the crate, chase off a crow, sleep. Each day is a small run you get better at.

**Patchwork: the almanac puzzle.** 2D, wide camera, point at tiles, twelve moves a day with time frozen, synergy soil (moisture, nutrients, companions, quality), seasonal almanac goals with stars and a seed draft, wild weather with a three-day forecast, solo, one smart action. The fun is planning: where does the corn go so the beans can climb it and the lettuce gets its shade, with rain due on Thursday.

**Bushel & Co.: the farm that runs itself.** 3D, wide camera, point and drag, a continuous clock you can speed up, classic growth, town orders with deadlines and a market that moves, calm weather, a crew of farmhands, one smart action. The fun is the machine: hire hands, put down sprinklers, lay out fields to fill the orders, then speed up time and watch it work.

## Shared rules

- The farm is 20 by 15 tiles. Six fields of 4 by 4. The first is owned and tilled; the rest are bought from the notice board. A new field starts as meadow and needs tilling once. Tilled soil stays tilled.
- Eight crops. Seeds are paid for when planted. Harvests go to stock, then to the crate.
- Classic growth: a crop grows one day each night it was watered that day. Three dry nights in a row and it withers. Ripe crops wait and never wither.
- Synergy growth: a crop grows if moisture at nightfall meets its thirst (1, lettuce 2). Moisture drops by one each night, two in a heat wave. Quality from 0 to 3 is shown while it grows: +1 a companion next door, +1 a sunflower next door (bees), +1 rich soil (nutrient 2 or more), minus 1 spent soil (0), minus 1 crowded by two or more of the same crop, minus 1 if it ever went thirsty. Quality multiplies the price by 1, 1.25, 1.5 or 2. Harvests use nutrient; beans put it back on their tile and the four around it; empty soil recovers slowly.
- Rain waters every plot. Sprinklers water the 3 by 3 around them each morning (Sunup's stands up on a stone footing, a brass riser with a blue-capped spinning head; the old low pad with a pipe along the soil read as a dropped tool; they cost 200; Sunup's water for ten mornings, a season, then wear out, with a word on the morning card and "N mornings left" on the caption, `SPRINKLER_MORNINGS`: two 120-coin sprinklers had ended watering for good). Scarecrows keep crows off everything within three tiles (Sunup's within two: a five by five square, sure only beside it).
- Crows land on unprotected crops. Walk near one or tap it to scare it. Left alone, it eats the crop after two game hours (turns: at nightfall).
- Bugs appear on a few growing crops each wild morning and spread to the same crop next door (in Sunup to every growing neighbour of the same kind and half of the others). Bugged crops stop growing and die after four nights. Tend to pick them off.

## Switching

A switch never loses progress, moves the farmer into a wall, or strands a job.

- Day to Turns: moves left = the smaller share of time or stamina left. Turns to Day: the clock jumps to match moves used, stamina matches moves left. Flow keeps the clock where it is.
- Classic to Synergy: watered plots read as moist. Synergy to Classic: moist plots read as watered today.
- Crew off: hands go into the bunkhouse, their jobs go back on the board, no wages. Crew on: the first time, one hand joins for free.
- Wild to Calm: crows fly off and bugs go.
- Point to Walk: the farmer drops queued jobs (hands keep theirs under Crew).
- Almanac starts at the current season the first time it is switched on, with every crop already in the ground unlocked.

## Feedback for the designer

The mixer shows how long has been spent in each option, and a notes box stamped with the current mix. This is the lab: the game exists to find the best mix.

## Crop art

Each crop has its own small drawing (`src/ui/crops-art.ts`): brown ink outlines, flat fills, one highlight and a darker line for the detail, on a 32 grid so it reads at 16 px and stays crisp at 44. It stands in everywhere a crop is named in the interface: the seed picker, the chosen seed on the Seeds tool, request notes, the board's Seeds tab, the stock list and draft cards. No crop is a coloured dot any more.
